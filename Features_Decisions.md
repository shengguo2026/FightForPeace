# Fight for Peace — Features and Decisions

This is the living product and game-design decision log for *Fight for Peace*. It records confirmed requirements separately from proposals and unresolved questions. Detailed implementation planning will follow after the Combat Lab design is approved.

## Status labels

- **Confirmed** — explicitly selected as part of the game design.
- **Proposed** — recommended for discussion; not yet a requirement.
- **Deferred** — intended for a later milestone rather than the first Combat Lab release.
- **Planned** — approved as a future refinement module whose detailed design is not yet decided.

## 1. Game vision

- **Confirmed:** *Fight for Peace* is a browser-based 2D side-view action game inspired by classic arcade brawlers such as *Captain Commando*, *Knights of the Round*, and *Final Fight*.
- **Confirmed:** Its defining feature is a brawler structure with deeper fighting-game mechanics.
- **Confirmed:** The protagonists value peace and fight to protect their village, loved ones, and relatives from specific threats.
- **Confirmed:** Each stage has its own story, reason for fighting, environment, enemies, boss, items, weapons, attack patterns, and ending.
- **Confirmed:** The first content milestone is Stage 1. Later stages extend the same combat engine through data-driven content.

## 2. Narrative and localization

- **Confirmed:** The village's wise elder analyzes threats, establishes goals, and motivates the heroes.
- **Confirmed:** The elder will be visually based on a personal photo supplied by the creator.
- **Confirmed:** The elder appears in story sequences and/or stage introductions through illustrated images and localized text.
- **Confirmed:** English is the default language.
- **Confirmed:** Simplified Chinese and German are also supported.
- **Confirmed:** Codex-generated pictures and artwork contain no embedded text.
- **Confirmed:** Story sequences combine text-free artwork with localized subtitle overlays.
- **Confirmed:** Story text is stored separately from artwork so the same image can be reused in every supported language.
- **Proposed:** Story text should be referenced through stable localization keys.

## 2.1 Artwork policy

- **Confirmed:** Codex will be used to generate the game's pictures and artwork.
- **Confirmed:** Generated images must not contain words, captions, labels, speech bubbles, or other language-dependent text.
- **Confirmed:** Localized titles, dialogue, narration, and subtitles are rendered by the game interface over or alongside the artwork.
- **Proposed:** Art prompts, source/reference images, output dimensions, style notes, and asset revisions should be tracked in an asset manifest so later stages remain visually consistent.

## 3. Heroes and progression

- **Confirmed:** Two heroes are initially selectable.
- **Confirmed:** Additional heroes become available as later stages are added and the story develops.
- **Confirmed:** Every hero has a unique set of attacks and combos.
- **Confirmed:** All heroes share universal movement, interaction, defense, hit-reaction, knockdown, and recovery rules while owning data-defined move lists and combo routes.
- **Confirmed:** The two founding heroes use the **Complementary Guardians** direction. They are distinct people with different village responsibilities and fighting identities, but both remain complete and viable in solo play rather than becoming narrow co-op classes.
- **Confirmed:** The working **Mountain Guardian** archetype is a calm, grounded, protective village craftsperson and waterworks keeper. Their combat identity emphasizes guard reads, close control, grounded routes, throws, stable launch setups, and deliberate power without reducing them to an immobile extreme grappler.
- **Confirmed:** The working **River Scout** archetype is a warm, alert, inventive village messenger and river-path guide. Their combat identity emphasizes mobility, aerial pursuit, repositioning, and converting launches without reducing them to a fragile extreme rushdown character.
- **Confirmed:** Their cooperative identity centers on readable setup, launch, pursuit, handoff, and finisher opportunities while retaining the approved universal co-op rules and independent combo contexts.
- **Confirmed:** Both heroes receive a dependable solo baseline that includes a quick starter, defensive answer, launcher or equivalent vertical-control tool, ground route, and reliable finisher. Character-specific strengths change how those tools function rather than removing essential answers.
- **Confirmed:** Hero Specials and Ultimates use a **Heightened Martial Energy** power language. Combat remains primarily physical, while exceptional techniques may manifest stylized energy, wind, water, pressure waves, impact force, or other character-specific expressions.
- **Confirmed:** Familiar fighting-game functions such as projectiles, rising anti-air attacks, advancing multi-hit spins, reversals, and enhanced signature techniques may be used as mechanical vocabulary. Every move receives original naming, animation, timing, trajectory, audiovisual treatment, tactical trade-offs, and character meaning rather than copying another game's complete technique.
- **Confirmed:** Not every hero must receive the same projectile–rising-attack–spinning-attack package. Each starting kit stays solo-viable while emphasizing the hero's own combat identity, and an Ultimate must add a distinct tactical payoff such as Resolve pressure, crowd control, lane movement, rescue space, or combo conversion rather than functioning only as a higher-damage Special.
- **Confirmed:** The two founders use **Character-Owned Complete Kits** rather than mirrored classic packages or extreme specialization. Their initial Special and Ultimate functions cover essential solo needs through different answers and create distinct freestyle-cancel and cooperative-handoff opportunities.
- **Confirmed:** The Mountain Guardian's initial kit uses the working moves **Stonewake Palm** (`U`), a short broad ground-pressure wave; **Gatebreaker Advance** (`Forward + U`), a committed armored drive; **Rising Rampart** (`Backward + U`), a rising anti-air and reversal; and **Rivergate Oath** (`H`), an intercept-and-launch Ultimate ending in a circular rescue shockwave with strong Resolve pressure.
- **Confirmed:** The River Scout's initial kit uses the working moves **Current Crescent** (`U`), a fast narrow pressure-wave projectile; **Gale Wheel** (`Forward + U`), an advancing multi-hit spinning kick; **Ebbstep Return** (`Backward + U`), an evasive retreat and counterattack; and **Tempest Crossing** (`H`), a projectile-led aerial pursuit and lane-shifting finishing sequence.
- **Confirmed:** The move functions and input relationships are fixed; the working move names may change during final character naming and localization. Exact frame data, damage, meter interaction, invulnerability or armor windows, cancel links, trajectories, hit reactions, and audiovisual treatment remain Combat Lab authoring and tuning work.
- **Confirmed:** Future store techniques and later-hero kits follow a **Function Inspiration Library** policy. Existing fighting games, including *Fatal Fury: City of the Wolves*, may be studied for universal tactical functions such as projectile control, rising anti-air, advancing carry, stance branching, defensive conversion, grabs, aerial follow-ups, and traps. Every shipped technique must be independently authored for *Fight for Peace* with its own village-rooted fiction, name, silhouette, animation sequence, timing, trajectory, hit behavior, audiovisual language, trade-offs, depth-axis behavior, and cooperative applications. Existing move names, character identities, distinctive animations, voice lines, effects, timings, or complete command packages are not copied.
- **Confirmed:** The Function Inspiration Library is an internal design and coverage-audit tool, not an in-game catalogue and not a promise that every observed function will be shipped. A candidate technique is admitted only when it fits a specific hero or enemy identity, fills a demonstrated tactical role, preserves sidegrade balance, and remains readable in the depth-axis cooperative battlefield.
- **Unresolved:** The heroes' final personal names, ages, genders, visual designs, detailed biographies, final purchased Special and directional-Ultimate catalogues, voiced mannerisms, and final weapon-affinity move data remain production character-design decisions. The founders' Representative Vertical Slice normal-action catalogues and teaching routes are confirmed, while **Mountain Guardian** and **River Scout** remain working archetype titles rather than confirmed personal names.

## 4. Game modes

- **Confirmed:** Single-player mode.
- **Confirmed:** Same-device co-op mode.
- **Confirmed:** Local Wi-Fi co-op mode.
- **Confirmed:** Co-op progression uses **Host-Authorized Play with Verified Personal Credit**. The host may select any globally unlocked difficulty on the host profile, and another player may join even when that tier is not yet unlocked on the joining profile.
- **Confirmed:** Same-device Player 2 may select another persistent local profile or temporary Guest mode. Local-Wi-Fi participants use profiles stored on their own devices.
- **Confirmed:** Each persistent participant records personal stage completion, score, rank, discoveries, best combo, and co-op status. A clear at a higher difficulty also counts as completion of that stage at all lower difficulties.
- **Confirmed:** Global tier advancement evaluates each profile's verified campaign-clear records regardless of which player hosted the contributing sessions. A single isolated high-tier stage cannot satisfy a campaign-wide tier requirement.
- **Confirmed:** Temporary Guest mode participates fully during the session but cannot store permanent progression. The results screen states exactly what each persistent profile recorded and whether it completed a global unlock requirement.
- **Confirmed:** Progression ownership never modifies combat simulation or grants host gameplay advantages. Disconnect, rejoin, and incomplete-session credit are defined by the later local-Wi-Fi recovery rules.
- **Confirmed:** Local-Wi-Fi synchronization uses a **Host-Authoritative Deterministic Simulation with Prediction and Bounded Rollback**. Solo, hot-seat, host, and client all use the same fixed-step gameplay simulation, provisionally targeting 60 simulation updates per second.
- **Confirmed:** Devices exchange frame-stamped abstract gameplay actions rather than raw device events. The host owns authoritative frame order, accepted inputs, random seed, spawn decisions, and canonical gameplay state while every peer simulates the same combat, AI, items, weapons, and encounter rules.
- **Confirmed:** Each device predicts its local player's input immediately and uses a small configurable input delay to absorb ordinary Wi-Fi variation. Late remote inputs inside an authored rollback window rewind and deterministically resimulate affected frames.
- **Confirmed:** Peers exchange periodic state hashes. On divergence, the host supplies a compact authoritative snapshot; severe visible correction is deferred to a safe presentation point when possible without allowing simulation disagreement to continue.
- **Confirmed:** Gameplay state is rollback-safe. Particles, camera shake, and similar presentation effects remain outside canonical state, while audiovisual events use stable identifiers so resimulation cannot replay the same hit, voice, or effect multiple times.
- **Confirmed:** Network quality monitoring may raise input delay within an authored safe range before declaring the session unstable. Solo and hot-seat omit networking and rollback overhead while retaining the same deterministic combat rules.
- **Confirmed:** Combat Lab network tests include artificial latency, jitter, packet loss, rollback, state-hash divergence, snapshot recovery, and duplicate-presentation-event detection.
- **Confirmed:** Local-Wi-Fi interruption recovery uses **Short Recovery Grace, Restricted Survival Proxy, and Host Migration**. A briefly missing player does not immediately end the encounter or hand unrestricted control to AI.
- **Confirmed:** As soon as remote input is judged missing, held movement and repeated inputs are cleared. The hero completes any already committed action and returns to a guarded neutral state during a short recovery grace.
- **Confirmed:** If the interruption outlasts that grace, a restricted survival proxy may follow the active hero, avoid hazards, block, evade, recover, and use conservative basic attacks when necessary. It cannot spend Super meter, initiate cooperative attacks, consume or throw items, collect or throw weapons, or make route, story, difficulty, progression, or other player-owned choices.
- **Confirmed:** The proxy uses the disconnected hero's real Health and Defense resources and receives no invulnerability. Encounter rules remain stable until an authored encounter or checkpoint boundary rather than immediately rescaling around the missing player.
- **Confirmed:** A returning peer receives and validates an authoritative snapshot, then regains control at the first safe actionable moment rather than interrupting a proxy action midway.
- **Confirmed:** A persistent guest who remains disconnected through the result receives only already confirmed statistics and checkpoint credit, not the final clear, rank, or unlock. A short result-screen recovery grace may still restore full completion credit when validation succeeds.
- **Confirmed:** If the host disconnects, the guest promotes its last confirmed deterministic frame to authority, discards unconfirmed frames through bounded rollback, and continues as the new host. A returning former host rejoins as the guest.
- **Confirmed:** When migration or resynchronization cannot be validated, the game preserves the last confirmed checkpoint and ends the network session with a clear explanation instead of guessing or granting unverified completion.
- **Confirmed:** Disconnect, proxy-control, migration, snapshot, rejoin, and credit events are recorded in the deterministic session log for replay, debugging, and progression verification.
- **Confirmed:** Local-Wi-Fi session discovery uses **Dual-Path Ephemeral Pairing**. The normal path creates a short-lived room code and QR code through a minimal signaling service; an offline fallback exchanges the complete WebRTC pairing package manually through two-way QR scanning or copy-and-paste.
- **Confirmed:** Pairing never requires a player account. Online room codes and pairing tokens are single-use, expire quickly, and carry only the temporary offer, answer, and connection-candidate messages needed to establish the session; they do not store gameplay, saves, or progression.
- **Confirmed:** After pairing, deterministic gameplay messages use a WebRTC `RTCDataChannel` peer connection whenever the browsers and network can establish it. Online and offline pairing feed the same host-authoritative gameplay protocol and do not create different combat rules.
- **Confirmed:** Before the lobby becomes ready, both peers validate the game version, networking-protocol version, deterministic-rules hash, and installed content manifest. A mismatch blocks the match with a specific corrective explanation rather than risking desynchronization.
- **Confirmed:** The lobby displays both selected heroes, assigned input devices, difficulty, connection quality, content compatibility, and readiness. The host can revoke the unstarted pairing, and each participant explicitly confirms readiness before gameplay begins.
- **Confirmed:** Offline pairing waits for candidate gathering and packages the required offer or answer and connection candidates into one transferable payload. The interface may split an oversized QR payload into an ordered sequence while always retaining copy-and-paste as an accessibility and device fallback.
- **Confirmed:** Pairing failures distinguish expired codes, incompatible versions or content, camera-permission problems, malformed payloads, unreachable peers, and restrictive or client-isolated Wi-Fi. The game does not promise universal zero-input LAN discovery from browser APIs.
- **Confirmed:** Pause and browser-focus recovery use a **Deterministic Courtesy Hold with Bounded Recovery**. Solo and same-device play pause immediately when the game window loses focus, becomes hidden, or an assigned controller disconnects.
- **Confirmed:** In local-Wi-Fi play, unexpected focus loss or page hiding first clears held inputs and requests a short synchronized courtesy hold at the latest mutually confirmed simulation frame, provisionally about three seconds. Canonical combat timers and resources stop advancing while connection monitoring and recovery messages remain active.
- **Confirmed:** A player who returns during the courtesy hold must explicitly confirm readiness. Both devices resume from the same confirmed frame after a synchronized countdown; merely making the tab visible again never restarts combat automatically.
- **Confirmed:** When the courtesy hold expires, the session enters the approved interruption-recovery sequence instead of remaining frozen indefinitely: recovery grace, restricted survival proxy, or host migration as applicable.
- **Confirmed:** Either local-Wi-Fi participant may request an explicit synchronized pause, which begins at a deterministic safe boundary. During it, players may change local controls, audio, subtitle, display, and accessibility settings, but cannot change difficulty, combat rules, progression ownership, or other shared match conditions.
- **Confirmed:** Pausing clears stale gameplay inputs and does not generate meter, reduce cooldowns, age defensive timing windows, advance AI, or create any other simulation advantage. Resume begins from neutral input state after the synchronized countdown.
- **Confirmed:** Combat Lab reliability tests cover window blur, tab hiding, minimization, controller loss, repeated visibility changes, host suspension, courtesy-hold expiry, manual pause, synchronized resume, and transition into proxy or migration recovery.
- **Confirmed:** Performance handling uses **Fixed Simulation with Independent Adaptive Presentation**. The deterministic combat simulation retains its validated fixed rate, provisionally 60 Hz, while each device renders independently at the frame rate it can sustain.
- **Confirmed:** Presentation may omit or interpolate visual frames without dropping, stretching, or redefining simulation frames. Exact Block windows, counters, cancels, hitboxes, movement, AI, cooldowns, and all other gameplay timing remain identical across display refresh rates and quality settings.
- **Confirmed:** Auto Quality is enabled by default, with manual quality levels available. Automatic reductions follow an authored order across decorative background animation, nonessential particles and debris, post-processing, lighting effects, and internal rendering resolution.
- **Confirmed:** Adaptive quality never removes or delays gameplay-critical silhouettes, attack telegraphs, directional warnings, HUD information, subtitles, hit confirmation, or essential sound cues.
- **Confirmed:** Simulation catch-up work is bounded so a slow device cannot enter an unlimited backlog spiral. Temporary performance debt lowers presentation quality first, then uses bounded catch-up or an authoritative snapshot when necessary.
- **Confirmed:** Both devices run a short simulation and rendering preflight before a local-Wi-Fi match. Persistent performance failure triggers a synchronized courtesy hold and a clear diagnostic rather than silently slowing combat.
- **Confirmed:** Lobby and progression ownership are separate from technical simulation authority. If the authoritative device remains unstable while its peer is healthy, authority may migrate to the healthier device without changing difficulty access, save ownership, or completion-credit ownership.
- **Confirmed:** If neither device can sustain the validated simulation requirements, the session preserves the last confirmed checkpoint and ends safely instead of corrupting synchronization or changing combat speed.
- **Confirmed:** Combat Lab performance tests cover 30, 60, 120, and 144 Hz presentation, artificial CPU load, dropped render frames, bounded catch-up, thermal slowdown, resizing, fullscreen changes, independent quality transitions, authority migration, and dual-device failure.
- **Deferred:** Local Wi-Fi synchronization is implemented after the solo and same-device combat simulations are stable.
- **Unresolved:** Final simulation rate validation, minimum simulation headroom, presentation-quality thresholds and reduction order, catch-up limits, preflight duration and pass criteria, authority-performance scoring, initial and maximum input delay, rollback-window size, input-prediction policy, hash cadence, snapshot format, safe correction points, network-quality thresholds, concrete `RTCDataChannel` configuration, signaling-service implementation, pairing-payload format and QR limits, optional relay policy, heartbeat timeout, recovery-grace duration, proxy behavior and cooldowns, migration timeout, result-screen recovery grace, checkpoint-credit boundaries, snapshot/rejoin presentation, courtesy-hold duration, safe pause boundary, resume acknowledgements and timeout, manual-pause limits, and exact focus-loss policy remain networking design and test values. The Host-Authoritative Deterministic Simulation with Prediction and Bounded Rollback, Short Recovery Grace with Restricted Survival Proxy and Host Migration, Dual-Path Ephemeral Pairing, Deterministic Courtesy Hold with Bounded Recovery, and Fixed Simulation with Independent Adaptive Presentation models themselves are confirmed.

## 5. Platform and input

- **Confirmed:** The game runs in a web browser.
- **Confirmed:** Keyboard and gamepad input are supported.
- **Confirmed:** The initial Player 1 keyboard concept is:
  - `W/A/S/D`: movement and directional modifiers.
  - `T`: attack and directional attack/combo inputs.
  - `Z`: jump and aerial attack/combo inputs.
  - `U`: character-specific special moves, modified by direction.
  - `G`: guard-system actions, modified by direction where applicable.
  - `H`: super attack when the attack meter permits it.
  - `J`: grab, throw, pick up food, pick up weapons, and throw equipped weapons.
- **Confirmed:** The listed keyboard bindings are defaults rather than fixed controls.
- **Confirmed:** Players can reassign keyboard and gamepad controls in the game settings.
- **Confirmed:** Default letter bindings follow the character printed by the active keyboard layout. For example, the German QWERTZ key labeled `Z` triggers Jump even though its physical browser code is `KeyY`.
- **Confirmed:** Physical keyboard keys and gamepad controls map to a shared set of abstract gameplay actions. Combat rules do not depend directly on a particular device or key.
- **Confirmed:** Special moves use a **Five-Slot Progressive Special Layout**. Neutral `U` always performs the hero's fixed signature Special and cannot be replaced.
- **Confirmed:** `Forward + U` and `Backward + U` begin with hero-specific core Specials and may later be reassigned to compatible purchased Specials. `Up + U` and `Down + U` begin empty and may be filled with compatible purchased Specials.
- **Confirmed:** Purchasing or reassigning a Special never deletes the hero's original moves. Owned Specials may be re-equipped without another purchase, and an empty optional slot performs no substitute attack.
- **Confirmed:** `Forward` and `Backward` are resolved relative to the hero's facing when the `U` input is accepted. `Up` and `Down` retain their stage-depth meaning, and Assisted Facing cannot silently redirect the selected Special.
- **Confirmed:** Direction-plus-button inputs use a deterministic cardinal-direction resolver shared by keyboard and gamepad. Keyboard ties favor the most recently pressed cardinal direction; analog input uses the dominant axis after dead-zone processing. Exact dead zones and equal-axis tie behavior remain Combat Lab tuning values.
- **Confirmed:** Special assignments are saved per persistent profile and hero as part of named loadouts. The last-used loadout remains selected until the player changes it.
- **Confirmed:** Ultimate moves use a **Progressive Five-Choice Ultimate Loadout with a Shared Full Meter**. Neutral `H` always performs the hero's fixed signature Ultimate and cannot be replaced.
- **Confirmed:** `Forward + H`, `Backward + H`, `Up + H`, and `Down + H` are initially empty assignable slots for compatible purchased Ultimates. The inputs exist from the beginning, while their move content is added progressively rather than requiring five Ultimates per hero at launch.
- **Confirmed:** Every Ultimate choice uses the same Attack Bar. Activating any Ultimate consumes the full bar and makes all other Ultimate inputs unavailable until the bar is refilled; one Ultimate cannot cancel or chain directly into another.
- **Confirmed:** Directional Ultimates are tactical sidegrades rather than upgrades to the signature move. They may emphasize advancing assault, defensive reversal or rescue, depth repositioning, lane control, area pressure, or another authored role with explicit trade-offs.
- **Confirmed:** Each acquired Ultimate declares compatible directional slots and cannot occupy multiple slots in the same loadout. Owned Ultimates are permanent and may be re-equipped without another purchase.
- **Confirmed:** An empty directional Ultimate slot does not fall back to neutral `H`, preventing an unintended Attack-Bar expenditure. The game supplies brief audiovisual feedback when a full-meter directional input addresses an empty or incompatible slot.
- **Confirmed:** Ultimate direction resolution uses the same deterministic input rules as Specials: the direction is captured when `H` is accepted, forward/backward use the hero's facing at that moment, up/down retain stage-depth meaning, and Assisted Facing cannot change the selected slot.
- **Confirmed:** Browser compatibility uses **Tested Support Tiers with Capability Gates**. The game is desktop-first because its primary controls require a keyboard or gamepad.
- **Confirmed:** Tier 1 browsers receive complete validation for solo, same-device co-op, local-Wi-Fi co-op, keyboard, gamepad, audio, saves, rendering, and performance. The initial Tier 1 goal covers current desktop Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari, with exact supported versions published and revised through the compatibility test process.
- **Confirmed:** A Tier 2 browser may run the game when its capability preflight succeeds, accompanied by a clear best-effort support notice. Mobile browsers and embedded webviews are not initially promised complete gameplay support.
- **Confirmed:** Compatibility is evaluated by required capabilities rather than browser-name rejection alone. The preflight independently checks rendering, Web Audio, keyboard and gamepad input, browser database storage, WebRTC data channels, page visibility, optional camera access, and optional fullscreen support.
- **Confirmed:** Missing optional capabilities disable or replace only their dependent feature. For example, denied camera access preserves copy-and-paste offline pairing, and missing fullscreen support preserves windowed play.
- **Confirmed:** Missing WebRTC disables local-Wi-Fi mode without blocking solo or same-device play. A missing capability required by the selected mode produces a localized explanation before a stage begins.
- **Confirmed:** Browser-specific workarounds remain isolated behind platform adapters and cannot alter deterministic combat data, timing, hit detection, AI, progression, or other gameplay rules.
- **Confirmed:** Combat Lab compatibility validation combines an automated browser report with manual controller, audio-latency, fullscreen, save-storage, and local-network tests on the published Tier 1 matrix.
- **Unresolved:** Exact input-buffer and chord windows, analog dead zones, equal-axis tie behavior, per-hero Special- and Ultimate-slot compatibility, initial directional-Ultimate inventory, empty-slot feedback, browser-version ranges, operating-system coverage, minimum rendering capability, Tier 2 warning language, mobile experimentation, and the compatibility-test device matrix remain implementation and validation decisions. The Five-Slot Progressive Special Layout, Progressive Five-Choice Ultimate Loadout with a Shared Full Meter, and Tested Support Tiers with Capability Gates models themselves are confirmed.

## 6. Combat system

### 6.1 Combat categories

- **Confirmed:** Standing attacks and combos.
- **Confirmed:** Aerial attacks and combos.
- **Confirmed:** Ground attacks and combos.
- **Confirmed:** Character-specific special moves.
- **Confirmed:** Super attacks require a full attack meter.
- **Confirmed:** The attack meter is refilled through successful attacks and combos.

### 6.2 Ground combat

- **Confirmed:** Ground combat uses the **downed follow-up** model.
- **Confirmed:** Heroes can perform brief attacks, grabs, or submission-style actions against a knocked-down enemy.
- **Confirmed:** Ground interactions return the participants to normal brawler combat rather than entering a separate, persistent grappling mode.
- **Confirmed:** Knockdown and ground follow-ups use a Tagged Two-Step Ground Route. A knockdown move declares either `terminalKnockdown`, which immediately enters protected recovery, or `opensGroundWindow`, which creates an authored opportunity for eligible ground combat.
- **Confirmed:** Ground-route entry uses a **Context-Sensitive Ground-Opportunity Focus** rather than a separate ground-combat mode or dedicated button. When the hero is grounded, close enough, and facing a marked target whose ground window is open, `T` and eligible directional-`T` inputs resolve to that hero's ground actions. Without that valid focus they retain their ordinary standing meanings; while airborne they retain their aerial meanings.
- **Confirmed:** The target that opened the ground window receives a clear opportunity marker and a short soft focus. Moving or facing away releases that focus so the player can attack a standing threat instead. A cooperative partner may claim the same marked target by entering its ground range, but both heroes share the target's one extension-and-finisher allowance.
- **Confirmed:** An open ground window permits at most one optional `groundExtension` followed by one `groundFinisher`. The attacker may skip the extension and use a finisher immediately.
- **Confirmed:** A multi-hit ground move counts as one grounded offensive action. A ground finisher closes further ground cancels and concludes with protected get-up or forced separation.
- **Confirmed:** A ground relaunch counts immediately as the ground finisher and consumes both the combo's ordinary ground-bounce quota and the move's authored juggle cost.
- **Confirmed:** Re-knocking the target down does not refresh the ground route. Both cooperative heroes share the target's ground-route state, allowing authored handoffs without granting extra extensions.
- **Confirmed:** Missing the authored ground window leads directly to protected recovery. Only explicitly tagged moves can strike a downed target; ordinary attacks cannot accidentally reset or extend the route.
- **Confirmed:** Damage scaling and hit-stun decay continue throughout ground combat. Large enemies and bosses may reject submissions or ground relaunches except during authored vulnerable states.
- **Unresolved:** Exact ground-window durations, extension and finisher tags, protected-get-up timing, and target-specific submission eligibility remain tuning values. The Tagged Two-Step Ground Route model itself is confirmed.

### 6.3 Defense

- **Confirmed:** Normal guard reduces incoming damage and consumes the defense meter.
- **Confirmed:** A hero cannot use normal guard while the defense meter is empty.
- **Confirmed:** Exact block is triggered by guarding at the moment an attack lands. It can create an offensive opportunity and refill defense meter.
- **Confirmed:** Counterattack is a higher-reward defensive action that can inflict significant damage and refill defense meter.
- **Confirmed:** Defense-meter exhaustion uses Controlled Guard Crush. When a blocked enemy action exhausts the remaining defense meter, that action still receives normal guard damage reduction and the meter becomes zero.
- **Confirmed:** Controlled Guard Crush places the hero in a short stagger, provisionally 24–30 frames at 60 Hz. The guard-crush reaction itself deals no additional damage and does not launch or knock down the hero.
- **Confirmed:** A subsequent enemy hit may punish the guard-crush stagger and begins an ordinary target-owned combo context governed by the universal damage scaling, hit-stun decay, juggle, ground-route, breaker, and protected-recovery rules.
- **Confirmed:** Simultaneous legal attacks may apply their combined authored guard damage, but exhausting the meter produces only one guard-crush reaction rather than stacking multiple staggers.
- **Confirmed:** Voluntary defense-meter spending, including a combo breaker, never triggers Guard Crush. Guard Crush occurs only when incoming guard damage exhausts the meter.
- **Confirmed:** After guard-crush stagger ends, normal guard remains unavailable while defense meter is zero, but a correctly timed exact block or counterattack remains possible under the approved defense-recovery model.
- **Confirmed:** Guard Crush receives distinct meter, visual, sound, and character-reaction feedback so players can immediately recognize the state change.
- **Unresolved:** Final guard-crush duration, simultaneous-hit guard-damage handling, audiovisual presentation, and attacker cancel opportunities after the crushing action remain Combat Lab tuning values. The Controlled Guard Crush model itself is confirmed.
- **Confirmed:** Exact-block timing uses a Consistent Core Window with Limited Authored Exceptions. A fresh Guard press opens one exact-block attempt; holding Guard does not repeatedly generate new exact-block attempts.
- **Provisional:** The standard exact-block window is five fixed-simulation frames at 60 Hz and runs immediately before contact through the contact frame. There is no after-impact correction.
- **Confirmed:** Pressing Guard too early transitions into normal guard if the hero has defense meter. Pressing after the contact frame does not retroactively block an attack that has already connected.
- **Confirmed:** An unsuccessful exact-block attempt creates a short precision cooldown that prevents rapid Guard-button tapping from repeatedly opening exact-block windows while leaving ordinary held guard available.
- **Confirmed:** Most enemy attacks use the standard timing. A rare, clearly telegraphed action may declare a slightly wider or narrower exact-block window as an explicit move-data property rather than receiving an undocumented exception.
- **Confirmed:** Difficulty levels do not secretly change the exact-block window. Academy lessons and Combat Lab diagnostics may expose an action's timing and any authored exception.
- **Confirmed:** Exact-block evaluation uses the real button-press timestamp mapped into the fixed combat simulation. Offensive input buffering, frame presentation, and holding Guard cannot enlarge the approved precision window.
- **Unresolved:** The final standard frame count, failed-attempt precision cooldown, and permitted range for rare authored timing exceptions remain Combat Lab tuning values. The Consistent Core Window with Limited Authored Exceptions model itself is confirmed.
- **Confirmed:** Exact-block payoff uses Tagged Deflect Advantage. A successfully exact-blocked contact inflicts no health damage and consumes no defense meter.
- **Confirmed:** The provisional 15% defense-meter reward is granted at most once per incoming enemy action. Visually multi-hit actions therefore cannot become defense-meter farms.
- **Confirmed:** Exact-blocking an ordinary single-hit action produces a brief attacker deflect recoil and lets the defending hero recover first, creating a player-controlled punish opportunity rather than an automatic damaging response.
- **Confirmed:** Exact-blocking one contact of an authored multi-hit action does not normally cancel the entire action. The defender must guard, exact-block, evade, or counter its remaining legal contacts.
- **Confirmed:** An authored heavy, committed, or concluding contact may carry `deflectBreak`. Exact-blocking that contact stops the remaining sequence and produces a stronger recoil than an ordinary deflection.
- **Confirmed:** Exact block itself never deals automatic damage. Counterattack remains the separate higher-risk defensive mechanic that inflicts significant damage.
- **Confirmed:** Large enemies and bosses may use authored armor that replaces full recoil with a shorter advantage response, but a successful exact block still lets the defender recover first and receives clear audiovisual confirmation.
- **Confirmed:** A cooperative partner may exploit exact-block recoil through ordinary combat. The recoil becomes a formal cooperative handoff only when the involved actions carry the existing required handoff tags.
- **Confirmed:** Ordinary and sequence-breaking deflections use distinguishable hit-stop, sound, visual, and character-reaction feedback.
- **Unresolved:** Final recoil and advantage durations, which enemy contacts carry `deflectBreak`, boss armor responses, and exact audiovisual presentation remain Combat Lab tuning values. The Tagged Deflect Advantage model itself is confirmed.
- **Confirmed:** Counterattack activation uses a Directional Predictive Counter. The default gesture is the direction toward the incoming attacker combined with a fresh Guard press shortly before contact.
- **Confirmed:** The counter direction is evaluated in the belt-scrolling combat plane, including horizontal and scene-depth positioning, rather than using only the hero's left-or-right sprite facing.
- **Provisional:** The standard counter window is three fixed-simulation frames, making it narrower and more committed than the provisional five-frame exact-block window.

### 6.4 Combo safety

- **Confirmed:** Neither heroes nor enemies may perform infinite combos.
- **Confirmed:** Infinite-combo prevention is enforced by universal combat rules including hit-stun decay, damage scaling, juggle limits, knockdown and ground-route limits, and protected recovery states.
- **Confirmed:** Heroes can activate a combo breaker with `G + H` while trapped in hit-stun.
- **Confirmed:** A hero needs at least 75% defense meter to activate a combo breaker, and activation consumes 75% defense meter.
- **Confirmed:** A combo breaker deals no direct damage, pushes nearby enemies away, and returns combat to neutral.
- **Confirmed:** A hero can use at most one combo breaker during an incoming combo.
- **Confirmed:** Combo-breaker activation uses a State-Gated Buffered Breaker. `G + H` is accepted during ordinary hit-stun, an active aerial juggle, or an open ground-follow-up sequence.
- **Confirmed:** Breaker input entered during hit-stop is queued only until the first legal simulation frame immediately afterward. It does not require a second precision window beyond being in an eligible trapped state.
- **Confirmed:** A breaker is unavailable during ordinary neutral control, block-stun, Guard Crush, protected recovery, defeat, and stage-transition states.
- **Confirmed:** Authored grabs, submissions, and cinematics do not permit the standard breaker unless their later escape rules explicitly opt into it.
- **Confirmed:** Activation atomically validates at least 75% defense meter and the target combo context's unused breaker allowance before spending meter or changing state.
- **Confirmed:** A successful breaker immediately closes the incoming target-owned combo context, protects the hero for the complete breaker animation, and pushes hostile bodies inside its approved radius to safe separation without dealing damage.
- **Confirmed:** Ordinary enemies receive standard breaker pushback. Bosses receive an authored separation reaction but cannot ignore the successful hero escape.
- **Confirmed:** A breaker affects every hostile body in its approved radius so another participating enemy cannot remain inside the space the breaker is intended to create.
- **Confirmed:** A hero cannot activate a breaker on behalf of a cooperative partner. Each trapped player controls that hero's own breaker decision and pays that hero's own defense meter.
- **Confirmed:** Invalid or unavailable breaker inputs consume nothing and are discarded rather than activating later after the hero returns to neutral.
- **Confirmed:** Breaker validation, cost, affected targets, and resulting separation are deterministic in same-device and local Wi-Fi cooperative play.
- **Unresolved:** Final breaker radius, animation protection, ordinary-enemy pushback, boss separation reactions, hit-stop input tolerance, and later grab or submission exceptions remain Combat Lab tuning values. The State-Gated Buffered Breaker model itself is confirmed.
- **Confirmed:** Ordinary enemies cannot use combo breakers.
- **Confirmed:** Bosses use a visible, predictable resolve burst after reaching a stun threshold rather than using the hero combo-breaker rule.
- **Confirmed:** Combo breakers supplement rather than replace universal anti-infinite rules such as hit-stun decay, damage scaling, juggle limits, limited ground follow-ups, and protected recovery.
- **Confirmed:** Protected recovery uses Context-Aware Protected Re-entry with three universal contexts: protected get-up after a terminal ground knockdown, air recovery leading to protected landing after juggle exhaustion, and a grounded soft reset after hit-stun exhaustion.
- **Confirmed:** Entering protected recovery immediately closes the previous combo context. During protection, the recovering fighter cannot take damage, hit-stun, launch, knockback, or grabs and cannot provide attackers with hit-confirmed cancels, meter, combo credit, handoffs, or continuation state.
- **Confirmed:** Heroes have two universal recovery choices: a faster neutral recovery in place or a slightly slower directional recovery that provides limited repositioning but cannot attack during its movement. There is no universal delayed wake-up.
- **Confirmed:** During the final recovery frames, a hero may buffer movement, normal guard, or an eligible character-specific reversal. Normal guard may become active on the first vulnerable frame, while exact block and counter still require a real correctly timed press and cannot be pre-buffered.
- **Confirmed:** There is no free universal invincible wake-up attack. A move tagged `wakeupEligible` begins only when ordinary recovery protection ends; any following invulnerability belongs to that move's authored properties, and Specials or Supers pay their normal resource costs.
- **Confirmed:** An invalid or unavailable reversal spends no resource and may fall back to an otherwise valid buffered guard or movement action. Recovery protection never overlaps the active attack frames of an ordinary wake-up action.
- **Confirmed:** Soft separation prevents allies or enemies from trapping a recovering fighter inside overlapping collision bodies. Directional recovery cannot pass through solid stage geometry or leave the playable area.
- **Confirmed:** Enemies use the same recovery framework, with AI selecting valid recovery choices. Boss-specific recovery behavior remains authored and visibly telegraphed rather than receiving hidden extra protection.
- **Unresolved:** Final protection durations, directional-recovery distance and timing, eligible reversal tags, and AI recovery selection weights remain tuning values. The Context-Aware Protected Re-entry model itself is confirmed.

### 6.5 Cooperative combat

- **Confirmed:** Co-op heroes can perform coordinated attacks against a single enemy for high damage.
- **Confirmed:** Cooperative combos use Organic Tagged Handoffs plus Authored Team Finishers. Normal handoffs emerge from each hero's regular character-specific move and cancel graph, while spectacular synchronized or cinematic team attacks remain deliberately authored moves.
- **Confirmed:** A compatible launcher or continuation may open a cooperative handoff opportunity. If the partner's eligible move connects while the target is still in the required combat state, that partner continues the existing target-owned combo context without requiring a separate handoff button.
- **Confirmed:** Cooperative handoffs use Authored Target-State Windows plus a Short Pre-Input Buffer. Sender moves declare an appropriate handoff state, such as airborne, wall rebound, standing stagger, or limited ground bounce, together with an authored opportunity window.
- **Confirmed:** The receiving hero may buffer an eligible action shortly before the handoff window opens. The provisional buffer is 6–8 frames at 60 Hz; it queues the player's action but does not extend enemy vulnerability, reposition either fighter, or guarantee that the attack connects.
- **Confirmed:** A receiving move claims the handoff only when it physically connects while the target remains in the required state. The opportunity closes on a successful claim, authored expiry, the target becoming actionable, protected recovery, a hero combo breaker, or a boss resolve burst.
- **Confirmed:** Academy and Combat Lab views may expose the exact handoff state and timing. Normal play communicates an available opportunity through restrained audiovisual cues rather than diagnostic text.
- **Confirmed:** A cooperative handoff never resets damage scaling, hit-stun decay, juggle count, ground-follow-up limits, dash-bridge use, or other anti-infinite state. The receiving hero continues through that hero's own authored combo routes.
- **Confirmed:** Both heroes may attack the same target, but the target resolves only one dominant reaction state at a time so overlapping hits cannot create contradictory launch directions or duplicate state transitions.
- **Confirmed:** Same-simulation-frame legal hits against one target form a Combined Hit Bundle. Hits that arrive on later simulation frames are evaluated normally against the target's resulting state rather than being pulled backward into the bundle.
- **Confirmed:** Every legal attack in the bundle contributes its damage, combo impact count, authored damage-scale cost, and authored hit-stun-budget cost. Each hero receives only that hero's own participation credit and meter gain.
- **Confirmed:** Same-frame attacks calculate damage from the shared scale present at the start of that simulation frame; afterward, all participating actions apply their normal scale and stun costs. This avoids player-slot ordering advantages without resetting or bypassing anti-infinite rules.
- **Confirmed:** A bundle produces one shared hit-stop event and one dominant target reaction. Reaction priority is: authored team finisher; valid cooperative handoff receiver; special forced reaction such as wall rebound or ground bounce; launcher or knockdown; heavy normal; then standard or light reaction.
- **Confirmed:** Equal-priority conflicts use a stable deterministic neutral tie-breaker derived from combat state and action identity, never player number, host status, or packet-arrival order. Replays, Academy recordings, and local Wi-Fi peers therefore resolve the same bundle identically.
- **Confirmed:** If the combined bundle exhausts the target's remaining anti-infinite budget, the appropriate forced-separation reaction overrides the otherwise selected reaction driver.
- **Confirmed:** Authored team finishers use Invite and Confirm with Equal Meter Contribution. An eligible hero initiates with a remappable abstract Team Attack action, and the partner must explicitly confirm during a provisional 30-frame response window; neither player is forced into participation.
- **Confirmed:** Each participating hero provisionally spends 50% attack meter, for a combined cost equal to one full attack meter. No separate team resource is introduced, and ordinary cooperative handoffs retain only their normal destination-move costs.
- **Confirmed:** A team-finisher opportunity requires an authored target state, a supported hero pairing, both heroes active and controllable, valid participation range, an unprotected target, and sufficient individual meter.
- **Confirmed:** On confirmation, the engine revalidates both heroes, the target, range, eligibility, and meter before atomically committing both costs and beginning the move. Declined, expired, interrupted, or invalid invitations spend no meter and force no animation.
- **Confirmed:** Simultaneous same-tick invitations merge into one request. Confirmation is resolved by simulation state rather than packet-arrival order so local Wi-Fi play remains deterministic.
- **Confirmed:** A successful team finisher remains subject to the shared damage-scaling system and may use an authored minimum floor. It concludes with protected separation or knockdown and closes the target's current combo context, preventing a further offensive extension.
- **Confirmed:** Friendly interaction uses Cooperative Safety with Soft Presence. Allied heroes retain soft body collision and gentle separation, but can pass through one another when needed and cannot permanently block movement, a dash, a landing, an escape route, or a narrow passage.
- **Confirmed:** Ordinary allied attacks cause no damage, hit-stun, knockback, launch, block reaction, interruption, or hit-stop. Their hitboxes pass through the allied hero and remain able to strike enemies beyond that hero.
- **Confirmed:** Contacting an ally generates no attack or defense meter, cancel confirmation, combo credit, handoff state, exact block, counter, or combo-break interaction. The partner cannot be used to farm resources or validate offensive routes.
- **Confirmed:** Ordinary grabs do not select allies. Hero-owned projectiles, thrown weapons, and area attacks pass through or ignore the partner without being consumed, while neutral stage hazards may affect both heroes.
- **Confirmed:** Only explicitly authored cooperative actions may reposition or animate an allied hero. Any such action that temporarily removes control requires both players to opt in through the cooperative-action rules.
- **Deferred:** A full-friendly-fire challenge modifier may be considered later; it is not the default and does not influence normal cooperative balance.
- **Confirmed:** Missing or arriving after a normal handoff opportunity does not preserve the combo artificially. The target proceeds into its ordinary fall, recovery, or protected state.
- **Confirmed:** Enemies can perform coordinated group attacks against heroes.
- **Unresolved:** Final pre-input-buffer length, per-move handoff durations, eligible sender and receiver tags, final authored reaction-priority values and neutral tie-break details, the Team Attack default binding, final confirmation duration and meter cost, pair-specific finishers and ranges, cinematic interruption rules, exact soft-collision tuning, and any later authored ally-reposition actions.

### 6.6 Resource economy

- **Confirmed:** Attack-meter generation uses Authored Per-Action Gain with Independent Combo Decay. Every offensive move defines `attackMeterGain`, and multi-hit moves distribute that authored total across their visual hits rather than receiving the full award per hit.
- **Provisional:** A confirmed hit awards 100% of the action's eligible gain, contact with guard awards 25%, and a whiff awards none. Protected targets, allies, and ordinary stage props award no attack meter.
- **Confirmed:** Each target-owned combo context contains an internal 100-point meter-gain budget. Successful offensive actions spend an authored `meterGainDecayCost`; multi-hit actions normally spend that cost once.
- **Provisional:** From 71 through 100 remaining budget, eligible actions award 100% meter; from 31 through 70 they award 60%; from 1 through 30 they award 25%; at zero they award none.
- **Confirmed:** The meter-gain budget is independent of damage scaling, hit-stun decay, and juggle control. Both cooperative attackers share the defending target's decay state, but each hero receives meter only from that hero's own successful actions; changing attackers does not refresh gain.
- **Provisional:** A move gains its normal award from the first valid enemy contact. Each additional unique enemy struck by the same action contributes a 25% crowd bonus, with total gain capped at 150% of the move's authored value.
- **Confirmed:** Supers and team finishers generate no attack meter from their own hits. Premium-cancel destination actions are provisionally tagged `noSelfRefund`, preventing an extension from immediately refunding its own resource cost.
- **Confirmed:** Failed or invalid attacks do not spend meter-gain budget. Attack meter is capped at 100%, overflow is discarded, heroes do not gain it passively or merely by taking damage, and defense meter remains separate.
- **Unresolved:** Final authored move gains, contact modifiers, meter-gain budget size and bands, crowd bonuses, and `noSelfRefund` coverage remain tuning values. The Authored Per-Action Gain with Independent Combo Decay model itself is confirmed.
- **Confirmed:** Defense-meter recovery uses Safety-Floor Recovery plus Skill-Earned Recovery. Every hero begins a stage or equivalent fresh combat start with 100% defense meter.
- **Confirmed:** Normal guard consumes the incoming action's authored `guardDamage`. Multi-hit actions normally define one total guard cost distributed across their visual hits rather than charging the complete cost for every visual hit.
- **Confirmed:** The existing combo-breaker rule continues to require at least 75% defense meter and consume 75% on activation.
- **Provisional:** A successful exact block restores 15% defense meter, while a successful counterattack restores 25%. Overflow beyond the 100% cap is discarded.
- **Confirmed:** When defense meter is below the 25% safety floor, passive recovery may begin after the hero has remained actionable for approximately 1.5 seconds without guarding or taking damage. Movement is permitted, while attacking, guarding, or taking damage resets the delay.
- **Confirmed:** Passive defense recovery stops at the 25% safety floor. Recovery above that floor must be earned through successful exact blocks, counterattacks, or another explicitly authored effect.
- **Confirmed:** At zero defense meter, normal guard is unavailable, but correctly timed exact blocks and counterattacks remain available so skilled defense can produce an immediate recovery. The passive safety floor prevents less experienced players from remaining permanently locked out of defense.
- **Confirmed:** Defense meter does not regenerate merely from dealing or receiving ordinary damage and remains separate from attack-meter generation.
- **Unresolved:** Final guard-damage values, exact-block and counterattack rewards, passive-recovery delay and rate, and any explicitly authored defense-restoring items remain Combat Lab tuning values. The Safety-Floor Recovery plus Skill-Earned Recovery model itself is confirmed.

### 7.1 Weapon moveset architecture

- **Confirmed:** Weapon combat uses Weapon Core with Hero-Affinity Overrides. Every weapon defines a complete `weaponCore` that all eligible heroes can use as a dependable fallback.
- **Confirmed:** A weapon core may define its standing attacks and combo routes, aerial attacks and routes, eligible ground attacks, reach, damage, guard damage, target reactions, throw behavior, resource values, and cancel tags.
- **Confirmed:** A hero may define a limited `weaponAffinity` override for selected weapon elements such as one directional branch, signature finisher, timing or reach adjustment, character-specific cancel, positioning behavior, or distinctive weapon throw.
- **Confirmed:** Equipping a melee weapon replaces the hero's ordinary `T` combo graph with the active weapon graph. Universal movement, guard, exact block, counterattack, recovery, and other compatible defensive systems remain available.
- **Confirmed:** Specials and Supers remain hero-owned. Each hero move explicitly declares whether it remains unchanged while armed, uses a weapon-compatible variant, or becomes temporarily unavailable with clear move-list feedback.
- **Confirmed:** Transitions among unarmed, weapon, aerial, ground, Special, and Super actions require explicit authored cancel links. Equipping a weapon never creates unrestricted cancellations.
- **Confirmed:** Weapon attacks use the same target-owned damage scaling, hit-stun decay, juggle control, ground-route limits, meter generation, and other anti-infinite rules as unarmed actions.
- **Confirmed:** The player-facing move list updates to show the active weapon core together with the selected hero's affinity techniques and temporarily unavailable hero moves.
- **Confirmed:** A newly added hero can use an existing weapon through its fallback core before any optional affinity overrides are authored, preventing hero and weapon additions from requiring a complete pair-specific moveset matrix.
- **Confirmed:** The first Combat Lab develops one complete melee weapon core and one simple throwable weapon before the weapon roster expands.
- **Confirmed:** Stage weapons use Authored Weapon-Lifecycle Profiles rather than one universal persistence or durability rule.
- **Confirmed:** A `temporaryMelee` weapon has authored integrity. Successful enemy contacts, guarded contacts, and weapon clashes may consume separately authored integrity amounts; whiffed attacks consume none.
- **Confirmed:** A `recoverableThrown` weapon pays authored integrity when thrown. If integrity remains after its resolved impact or landing, it becomes a stage pickup again; if the impact exhausts integrity, it breaks only after completing that collision.
- **Confirmed:** A `consumableThrowable` is intended for one resolved throw and then becomes embedded, destroyed, or otherwise unavailable according to its authored presentation.
- **Confirmed:** Integrity loss is deterministic and never random. Reaching zero allows the current offensive action to complete before the weapon breaks, so a weapon never disappears midway through an attack.
- **Confirmed:** Weapons communicate condition through readable stages such as Ready, Worn, and Critical, using both HUD information and visible or audible feedback rather than color alone.
- **Confirmed:** Reduced integrity does not silently reduce weapon damage. It communicates remaining lifespan only.
- **Confirmed:** Attack-contact cost, guard-contact cost, clash cost, and throw cost are authored independently in weapon data.
- **Confirmed:** After a weapon breaks, the user returns safely to the appropriate unarmed moveset on the next legal action. Enemies use the same weapon-lifecycle data when wielding stage weapons.
- **Confirmed:** Weapon loss caused by combat uses **Tagged Impact Disarming**. Every equipped weapon defines `gripResistance`, while only selected attacks or reactions define `disarmPower`.
- **Confirmed:** A forced disarm occurs only when the incoming action is explicitly eligible, its `disarmPower` meets the weapon's `gripResistance`, and the holder is in a legal disarm state. Ordinary light hitstun never disarms by default.
- **Confirmed:** Typical eligible sources are heavy knockdowns, launchers, powerful throws, selected Guard Crush attacks, and authored boss or weapon techniques. Eligibility remains data-driven per action.
- **Confirmed:** A successful normal guard, Exact Block, or Counter prevents the disarm. Guard Crush may disarm only when that specific attack is tagged to do so. Combo Breaker never drops the defender's weapon.
- **Confirmed:** The weapon is released only after the holder's hit reaction has established a safe trajectory, preventing it from spawning inside scenery or the holder. A dropped recoverable weapon preserves its current integrity unless the triggering action explicitly defines an impact-integrity cost.
- **Confirmed:** Once the dropped weapon lands, it becomes a stage pickup; temporary reclaim priority and later pickup competition are defined separately.
- **Confirmed:** Heroes, ordinary enemies, and bosses use the same disarm calculation. Bosses may receive high grip resistance or phase-specific disarm protection, but do not use a hidden exception system.
- **Confirmed:** Disarm resolution is deterministic in solo, hot-seat, and local-Wi-Fi play and receives distinct animation, sound, and HUD feedback so players can distinguish it from an intentional throw or weapon break.
- **Confirmed:** Weapon pickup ownership uses **Short Reclaim Priority, Then Neutral Contest**. A weapon lost through Tagged Impact Disarming is reserved for its former holder for 45 simulation frames after landing; afterward it becomes neutral to every compatible hero and enemy.
- **Confirmed:** Stage-spawned and container-released weapons are neutral immediately. An intentionally thrown recoverable weapon also becomes neutral when it lands, allowing a deliberate co-op weapon pass while preserving the risk that an enemy can claim it.
- **Confirmed:** The reclaim reservation ends immediately if the former holder is defeated, disconnects, leaves the playable area, or becomes unable to wield that weapon.
- **Confirmed:** Picking up a weapon always requires an explicit `J`/Interact input. Merely walking over a weapon never equips it, preventing accidental collection by a co-op partner or overlapping character.
- **Confirmed:** Simultaneous requests for a neutral weapon resolve deterministically by pickup eligibility, earliest input simulation frame, distance and alignment to the pickup, then a stable final tie-breaker. Player number, host privilege, and network packet-arrival order do not decide ownership.
- **Confirmed:** Weapon-compatible enemies and bosses may contest neutral weapons under the same ownership rules. AI pickup intent remains separately authored so every nearby enemy does not abandon combat automatically.
- **Confirmed:** Reserved and neutral pickup states use an icon plus localized text or another non-color-only cue. Ineligible actors receive no pickup animation or ownership transfer.
- **Confirmed:** Weapon integrity uses a **Layered Information Display**. The default combat HUD shows the equipped weapon icon and a compact Ready/Worn/Critical condition gauge instead of permanent exact durability numbers.
- **Confirmed:** Every condition transition is communicated redundantly through shape or icon changes plus animation and sound; color may reinforce the message but is never the only signal. Entering Critical produces a distinct imminent-break warning without predicting an uncertain future collision.
- **Confirmed:** Approaching an eligible stage weapon reveals its current condition before `J` is pressed, allowing an informed pickup without forcing players to equip it first.
- **Confirmed:** Academy and Combat Lab modes expose exact integrity, current and maximum values, condition thresholds, and the authored integrity costs of attack contact, guarded contact, clash, and throw actions.
- **Confirmed:** An optional **Detailed Combat HUD** may expose exact integrity values during ordinary stages. It is disabled by default and does not alter simulation, balance, AI knowledge, or network behavior.
- **Confirmed:** Enemy weapon-seeking uses **Role Permission plus Tactical Intent**. Each enemy archetype declares a pickup policy of `Never`, `Opportunistic`, or `Preferred`, in addition to weapon compatibility.
- **Confirmed:** `Opportunistic` enemies consider only nearby weapons that are safe enough to approach and meaningfully improve their current equipment. `Preferred` weapon specialists search more actively, while `Never` enemies preserve archetypes that should remain unarmed.
- **Confirmed:** Tactical pickup scoring may consider distance, weapon condition, compatibility, current equipment, nearby threats, and an encounter-authored limit on how many enemies should be armed. AI evaluates visible battle state and never reads future player inputs.
- **Confirmed:** Only one enemy receives temporary pickup intent for a particular neutral weapon at a time, preventing group pileups. This intent is not ownership: a hero may take the weapon first or interrupt the attempt.
- **Confirmed:** An enemy does not cancel an active attack, committed combo, defense action, or hit reaction merely to collect a weapon. Pickup intent is released if the weapon becomes unavailable, the enemy becomes ineligible, or the attempt exceeds its authored pursuit limits.
- **Confirmed:** Enemy pickup attempts are visibly telegraphed and punishable. Bosses use the same compatibility and ownership rules, with pickup interest authored per phase.
- **Confirmed:** AI pickup evaluation runs at fixed simulation intervals and resolves competing scores through stable tie-breakers, preserving deterministic behavior in solo, hot-seat, and local-Wi-Fi play.
- **Confirmed:** Integrity depletion uses a **Dramatic but Mechanically Safe Break**. The attack, guarded contact, clash, or throw that reduces integrity to zero resolves completely before the weapon is removed.
- **Confirmed:** A default weapon break does not automatically add damage, stagger, or self-damage. It receives brief hit-stop plus distinct animation, sound, and visual effects so the event feels consequential without punishing a successful final action.
- **Confirmed:** Break fragments are cosmetic: they have no hitboxes, damage, collision, or pickup behavior and disappear promptly. Rare story or magical weapons may later declare an explicit authored break effect rather than silently inheriting one.
- **Confirmed:** After the current action's normal recovery, the wielder transitions safely to the appropriate unarmed state and moveset. A thrown weapon depleted by its resolved collision breaks there and cannot return as a pickup.
- **Confirmed:** If integrity depletion and forced disarming would resolve from the same event, depletion has priority: the weapon breaks and cannot also create a recoverable dropped copy.
- **Confirmed:** Heroes, ordinary enemies, and bosses use the same break-resolution order. The deterministic event is recorded and presented as `weaponBreak`, distinct from an intentional throw or disarm.
- **Confirmed:** Melee weapon interactions use **Tagged Clashes with Authored Strength**. Only compatible `clashEnabled` offensive hitboxes can clash, and they do so only when their active regions meet before either attack reaches a hurtbox.
- **Confirmed:** Clash-enabled attacks declare an authored strength tier such as `Light`, `Heavy`, or `Crushing`. Equal-strength attacks deflect both wielders into short authored clash recovery; a stronger attack deflects the weaker action and continues its committed attack.
- **Confirmed:** Both participating weapons pay their authored clash-integrity cost. If that cost depletes a weapon, the clash outcome resolves first and the confirmed weapon-break sequence follows; a stronger final action may therefore complete before its depleted weapon is removed.
- **Confirmed:** The same pair of attack instances can clash only once, preventing multi-hit regions from repeatedly consuming integrity or trapping both actors in repeated recoil.
- **Confirmed:** Ordinary unarmed attacks do not clash with weapons. An unarmed Special, Counter, armored technique, or other move may participate only when explicitly tagged and given an authored clash strength.
- **Confirmed:** Thrown-weapon deflection is a separate response tag rather than an automatic property of every melee swing. `Crushing` or otherwise unclashable attacks require distinct audiovisual telegraphing.
- **Confirmed:** Heroes, enemies, and bosses use the same visible clash tags and strength comparison rather than hidden boss immunity. Clash strength, integrity cost, and recovery information are exposed in Academy and Combat Lab move data.
- **Confirmed:** Clash resolution uses simulation-frame contact ordering and stable priorities, ensuring identical outcomes in solo, hot-seat, and local-Wi-Fi play.
- **Confirmed:** Thrown attacks use **Tagged Projectile-Response Profiles**. A projectile can independently declare whether it is `blockable`, `exactDeflectable`, `counterReturnable`, `strikeDeflectable`, and, for rare authored techniques, `catchable`.
- **Confirmed:** Normal Block applies its usual reduced damage and Defense cost when the projectile is blockable, after which the projectile follows its authored result such as falling, harmless ricochet, embedding, or breaking.
- **Confirmed:** Exact Block prevents damage and grants its normal Defense reward against an eligible projectile, then deflects that weapon toward a safe neutral landing. It does not automatically damage or stagger a distant thrower.
- **Confirmed:** A successful Counter may redirect a `counterReturnable` projectile toward its attacker. The returned projectile becomes the countering actor's attack and follows normal target-owned damage scaling, hit-stun control, and meter rules.
- **Confirmed:** Only attacks explicitly tagged `projectileDeflect` can strike an incoming projectile aside. A weapon-based deflection may pay a separately authored integrity cost; ordinary attack-mashing does not provide universal projectile defense.
- **Confirmed:** Catching is not a universal `J` action. It is reserved for explicitly catchable projectiles and specifically authored hero, enemy, or boss techniques.
- **Confirmed:** Heavy, explosive, magical, and boss projectiles may deny selected responses, but their allowed defense profile must be telegraphed consistently and exposed through Academy and Combat Lab response icons.
- **Confirmed:** A recoverable weapon knocked to the ground becomes neutral under the confirmed pickup rules. Deflection and return trajectories use deterministic authored directions and speeds rather than random physics.
- **Confirmed:** Recoverable weapons use **Natural Landing with Deterministic Safety Correction**. A thrown, deflected, or disarmed weapon first follows its authored trajectory and resolves valid attack collisions normally.
- **Confirmed:** Before becoming collectible, the landing resolver validates ground support, collision clearance, playable-lane bounds, current encounter boundaries, hazard rules, and pickup reachability. A valid natural resting position is preserved exactly.
- **Confirmed:** When the natural position is invalid, the resolver tests a stable ordered list of nearby correction points and presents the movement as a visible bounce, skid, or ricochet toward the first legal result; correction candidates are never random.
- **Confirmed:** Safety correction cannot cross solid scenery, locked encounter boundaries, or move a weapon into a different combat region. During correction the weapon occupies a non-damaging, non-collectible `settling` state.
- **Confirmed:** The 45-frame reclaim window begins only after the weapon reaches its final valid resting position. If no nearby correction is legal, the weapon returns with clear audiovisual feedback to a stage-authored recovery anchor in the same combat region.
- **Confirmed:** Safety correction does not rescue a weapon from an explicitly destructive hazard when both the hazard and weapon lifecycle permit destruction. Stage validation flags regions that repeatedly require emergency recovery so this remains a safeguard rather than a substitute for sound level layout.
- **Confirmed:** Heroes, enemies, bosses, and every multiplayer mode use the same deterministic landing resolver.
- **Deferred:** Rare permanent or story-specific weapons may later use an explicit lifecycle profile; the first Combat Lab does not require them.
- **Unresolved:** The Canal Workstaff's and Iron Gate Handwheel's complete move data, whether any hero Specials or Supers require armed variants, initial integrity values and costs, concrete condition thresholds, concrete landing trajectories and correction search limits, stage recovery anchors and destructive-hazard assignments, concrete break audiovisuals and rare authored break effects, concrete AI pickup-policy assignments and scoring weights, encounter armed-enemy limits, concrete clash tiers, per-action clash tags, integrity costs and recovery values, per-projectile response profiles, deflection costs and trajectories, concrete catch techniques, concrete `gripResistance` and `disarmPower` values, legal disarm states, impact-integrity costs, and detailed disarm feedback remain later weapon-design and Combat Lab tuning decisions. The initial weapon identities, Canal Workstaff affinity direction, shared Handwheel behavior, Weapon Core with Hero-Affinity Overrides, Authored Weapon-Lifecycle Profiles, Tagged Impact Disarming, Short Reclaim Priority, Layered Information Display, Role Permission plus Tactical Intent, Dramatic but Mechanically Safe Break, Tagged Clashes with Authored Strength, Tagged Projectile-Response Profiles, and Natural Landing with Deterministic Safety Correction are confirmed.

### 7.2 Environmental combat and breakable scenery

- **Confirmed:** Environmental combat uses **Authored Deterministic Interactions** rather than unrestricted rigid-body physics. Heroes, enemies, elites, and bosses share the same core contact rules while authored resistance, Resolve, vulnerability, and phase data determine their reactions.
- **Confirmed:** A damaging environmental contact requires both an eligible move or reaction tag and sufficient remaining authored knockback force when the target reaches the surface. Merely touching scenery never creates combat damage.
- **Confirmed:** Passive impact damage combines a surface base value with a capped force contribution and remains inside the target-owned damage scaling, hit-stun, juggle, and anti-infinite systems. Active machinery and autonomous hazards use separate authored hazard actions and defense-response profiles.
- **Confirmed:** Breakable objects use authored integrity states such as **Intact**, **Damaged or Loosened**, **Critical**, and **Broken**. State changes are driven by authored `breakPower` and object thresholds rather than a permanently visible environment Health bar.
- **Confirmed:** Important states are readable through silhouette, cracks, motion, sound, captions where needed, and other non-color-only cues. Accessibility assistance may briefly emphasize a nearby eligible interaction without making every object glow permanently.
- **Confirmed:** Breakable scenery may create one interactive fragment at most unless an exceptional authored set piece explicitly declares otherwise. Other fragments are cosmetic, presentation-only debris.
- **Confirmed:** Interactive fragments reuse the existing Weapon Core through an `improvisedDebris` category. **Light** debris is throw-only; **Long** debris has one or two shared melee actions plus throw and limited integrity; **Heavy** debris has slow pickup and movement, one committed strike or throw, and may provide brief authored frontal protection.
- **Confirmed:** Compact debris profiles do not require the Canal Workstaff's complete standing, aerial, and ground graph. Stage 1 debris uses shared actions without hero-affinity overrides; later important debris may receive limited authored variations.
- **Confirmed:** Debris pickup, ownership, landing, checkpoint, co-op, clash, disarm, carry-boundary, and deterministic-recovery behavior uses the existing weapon lifecycle. `TRUSTYTOOLS` prevents applicable integrity loss but does not override consumed-on-impact behavior, destructive hazards, throws, drops, disarms, or authored region boundaries.
- **Confirmed:** Every target-owned combo context has one full passive `environmentImpactUsed` allowance. The first eligible passive collision may apply environmental target damage and consumes that allowance.
- **Confirmed:** A damaging environmental contact that creates a continuing wall rebound also consumes the ordinary wall-rebound quota. A contact that creates a ground bounce also consumes the ordinary ground-bounce quota. A terminal slam that enters protected separation consumes the environmental allowance without consuming a rebound quota that it does not use.
- **Confirmed:** Later passive contacts in the same continuous combo may show reduced contact, damage compatible scenery, or force safe separation, but cannot deal another full environmental target-damage event or refresh a bounce. Breaking scenery never resets scaling or another target-owned budget.
- **Confirmed:** Both heroes share the defending target's environmental and bounce allowances. Cooperative environmental setups use authored redirects and the same target-owned context; changing attackers never refreshes a quota or scaling.
- **Confirmed:** Bosses and resistant heavy enemies use **Resolve-Gated Environmental Reactions**. Outside an authored vulnerable state, an eligible contact applies capped Health damage, increased Resolve pressure, and a braced reaction without ordinary bounce or knockdown. During Stagger Break or another declared vulnerable phase, full authored slam, rebound, or knockdown reactions may occur.
- **Confirmed:** Authored Ultimates, cooperative finishers, and boss transitions may break several objects visually while calculating one bundled environmental damage event and then closing or safely resolving the combo.
- **Confirmed:** Active hazards do not consume the passive-impact allowance. They retain combo scaling, cannot reset a combo context, and use post-contact protection, forced separation, cooldown, or another authored safeguard so a target cannot be trapped for repeated unavoidable hazard damage.
- **Confirmed:** Hazard defense uses explicit profiles: standard defense, guard-pressure, deflectable, evade-oriented, or strongly telegraphed True Unblockable with a valid escape route. No hazard silently changes the universal Exact Block or Counter timing windows.
- **Confirmed:** Environmental persistence uses canonical region state and authored policies: persistent, encounter-restored, checkpoint-saved, consumed, story-controlled, or decorative cleanup. Interactive fragments remain region-bounded unless their data explicitly permits another boundary.
- **Confirmed:** An ordinary combat region normally contains at most one major environmental hazard or impact surface, two minor breakables, and one usable fragment at a time. Boss arenas may introduce additional elements by phase under the Threat Budget and effect budget.
- **Confirmed:** Functional objects are categorized as hostile structures, expendable combat objects, protected infrastructure, rescue-sensitive objects, or optional preservation objects. The game never assigns unavoidable blame, harsh mission failure, or Credit loss for required destruction; civilians are never projectiles; destroying scenery alone grants no score or meter.
- **Confirmed:** Meaningful environmental outcomes may award bounded score recognition, including tactical stuns, hazard avoidance followed by punishment, debris-assisted knockouts, cooperative redirects, boss Resolve breaks, or rescue-enabling destruction. One target can award the environmental bonus only once per continuous combo, repeated easy setups diminish or stop rewarding, and co-op recognition records both setup and execution.
- **Confirmed:** Environmental attacks build ordinary Attack Bar only through their legal damage and normal meter rules. Cheat-Assisted play grants no environmental score, Credits, achievements, grades, or mastery rewards.
- **Confirmed:** Difficulty preserves the same environmental rules and defensive windows. Higher profiles add enemy awareness, coordination, special roles, placements, and challenge combinations under the Threat Budget rather than multiplying environmental damage or hiding counterplay. `QUIETCROWD` reduces environmental commitment and concurrency while retaining selected-difficulty content.
- **Confirmed:** Environmental learning uses layered disclosure: a safe Stage 1 introduction, Academy practice, localized Guide reference, move-list eligibility tags, consistent non-color visual language, optional accessibility emphasis, transient success feedback, and Combat Lab diagnostics. No additional permanent HUD meter is introduced.
- **Confirmed:** Environmental artwork uses modular authored destruction. Important objects have intact, damaged, critical, and broken visuals; material families share behavior and animation templates; interactive fragments receive full pickup/use/impact states; cosmetic debris is pooled and recyclable. Adaptive quality may reduce cosmetic debris but never functional warnings, collision states, or interactive fragments.
- **Confirmed:** Validation combines deterministic automated tests, Combat Lab inspection, record-and-replay, local-Wi-Fi synchronization, checkpoint restoration, performance and accessibility tests, and playtesting for readability, satisfaction, accidental activation, and exploits.
- **Confirmed:** Stage 1 contains a **Representative Environmental Slice**: one authored impact surface, one multi-state breakable producing one usable fragment, one active mill or canal hazard, one cooperative redirect opportunity, one Resolve-gated boss interaction, a safe first-use introduction, one Academy exercise, and Combat Lab diagnostics. The Canal Workstaff and Iron Gate Handwheel remain its principal weapons.
- **Unresolved:** Exact environmental force thresholds, surface damage, break thresholds, hazard damage and protection windows, fragment move data, score values, Threat Budget costs, final object identities, and final artwork remain Combat Lab and Stage 1 content-authoring values. The system model and Representative Environmental Slice are confirmed.

### 7.3 Later-stage advanced ranged and explosive weapons

- **Confirmed:** Grenades, revolvers, machine guns, dedicated armed enemies, and their production artwork are **post-Stage 1 expansion content**. Stage 1 may reserve schema extension points or use optional placeholder Combat Lab experiments, but none is a Stage 1 acceptance requirement.
- **Confirmed:** Advanced weapons are scarce, finite-ammunition physical pickups found through authored containers, enemy drops, guarded racks, or stage events. They cannot be equipped before a stage, stored in the permanent inventory, or occupy Special or Ultimate loadout slots.
- **Confirmed:** Store content may supply cosmetic weapon variants or gallery material but cannot grant permanent firearm access, extra ammunition, or a mandatory power advantage.
- **Confirmed:** Ranged weapons use the existing abstract combat actions. `J` picks up the weapon; `T` and direction plus `T` use its authored attack graph; `J` with an occupied hand normally throws or discards it; `G`, compatible `U`, and compatible `H` retain their established meanings.
- **Confirmed:** Neutral firearm `T` uses a narrow deterministic soft-target cone. Forward, backward, up, and down modifiers select authored advancing, retreating, or depth-lane shots. A target indicator appears only when ambiguity requires it; the game does not become a permanent-crosshair or twin-stick shooter.
- **Confirmed:** While a firearm is held, a valid dash-state `T + G` chord retains priority and performs the hero's low attack without firing. `G` alone remains defense. The existing short deterministic chord-tolerance rules distinguish low attack from the firearm graph.
- **Confirmed:** At close range in a safe co-op handoff state, `J` opens a short transfer invitation and the partner confirms with `J`; without confirmation the carrier keeps the weapon. Directional `J` away from the partner forces an ordinary throw. Combat throws may use an authored partner-catch window.
- **Confirmed:** Grenade tap-and-release performs a quick throw. Holding `T` enters a bounded committed aim state with a landing-zone preview; release throws and direction adjusts the zone. Holding does not cook the fuse, and grenade aim cannot be freely Guard-cancelled.
- **Confirmed:** A machine-gun burst is one authored committed offensive action rather than an uncontrolled per-frame stream. Multi-bullet hits use one action-level combo, stun, juggle, and scaling treatment unless a separately cancellable phase is explicitly authored.
- **Confirmed:** The revolver is a precision-and-interruption tool with few strong shots and recovery that prevents rapid stun loops. The machine gun is a crowd-pressure tool with short bursts, lower per-hit damage, movement cost, recoil, and rapidly increasing escape resistance. Grenades are limited area-control tools with delayed, clearly warned explosions, strong knockback, and eligible environmental interactions.
- **Confirmed:** Weapon attacks participate in the existing target-owned combo context and never reset scaling, stun, juggle, bounce, breaker, or environmental state. Only authored `weaponCancellable` transitions can link into or out of ranged attacks. Grenades are normally high-commitment setup or finisher actions.
- **Confirmed:** Bosses convert excessive interruption into Resolve pressure under the existing phase and control rules. No firearm, burst, or grenade can create an infinite stun, juggle, environmental loop, or unavoidable boss pattern.
- **Confirmed:** Only dedicated authored enemy roles use firearms or explosives. They have distinct silhouettes, aiming or throw tells, warning zones, audio or caption cues, firing pauses, and Threat Budget costs. Enemy coordination increases by difficulty without removing established warnings.
- **Confirmed:** Standard eligible shots may be guarded at Defense cost and Exact Blocked for their authored advantage. Only explicitly tagged moves deflect or return projectiles. Grenade blasts are primarily evade-oriented; a legal block may reduce damage without erasing authored displacement.
- **Confirmed:** Each physical weapon owns its exact ammunition and transfers that amount when dropped, thrown, caught, or handed to another hero. Simultaneous pickup requests use deterministic proximity, facing, and stable tie-break rules; partners cannot steal directly from one another.
- **Confirmed:** Co-op uses one physical pickup rather than duplicating it. A grenade bundle may be divided only at an authored safe interaction. Friendly weapon attacks and explosions cause no allied gameplay damage, hit reaction, meter, or displacement; any allied blast movement is presentation-only.
- **Confirmed:** Weapons may cross nearby encounters inside an authored stage segment. Clearly warned narrative or traversal boundaries may require them to be left behind. Checkpoints save holder, type, position, condition, exact ammunition, grenade count, pickup state, and consumed resupplies without duplication.
- **Confirmed:** Ammunition uses **Compact Authored Profiles**. A revolver has a small fixed shot count and no ordinary manual reload; an empty revolver remains throwable. A machine gun has one finite ammunition pool, each burst spends its declared amount, and the empty weapon remains throwable. A grenade bundle is removed after its final grenade.
- **Confirmed:** A rare authored compatible resupply may restore ammunition only up to the weapon profile's maximum. Players have no separate permanent ammunition inventory, and collecting another firearm does not silently merge ammunition.
- **Confirmed:** Empty-fire input produces bounded dry feedback but no hit confirmation, meter, combo continuation, or repeatable sound spam. Firearms use ammunition rather than per-shot integrity as their primary depletion; throws, hazards, clashes, or authored events may still destroy them.
- **Confirmed:** `TRUSTYTOOLS` prevents applicable integrity loss but does not preserve or restore ammunition. No infinite-ammunition code is added to the initial cheat catalogue. Any future code must explicitly create Cheat-Assisted status and join the full cheat verification matrix.
- **Confirmed:** Higher difficulties preserve player weapon damage, controls, ammunition behavior, and defense rules while changing armed-enemy intelligence, contested pickup placement, optional acquisition routes, and authored combinations under the Threat Budget.
- **Confirmed:** Presentation is stylized, readable, and non-gory. Firearms are temporary protective tools rather than glamorous permanent equipment. Every critical warning uses at least two available channels, and players can reduce flashes, shake, recoil motion, explosive brightness, transient loudness, particles, and vibration without removing essential cues.
- **Confirmed:** Implementation follows **Architecture Hooks Now, Playable Content Later**. The Stage 1 architecture keeps extensible weapon, ammunition, targeting, cancel, ownership, checkpoint, and network schemas, but production encounters arrive in later stages. Optional placeholder Lab work is not a vertical-slice gate.
- **Confirmed:** Later validation covers deterministic targeting, ammunition, pickup arbitration, transfers, checkpoint state, combo scaling, resistance, environmental triggers, cheats, replay, local-Wi-Fi synchronization, reconnect recovery, accessibility, and performance before final media production.
- **Unresolved:** Exact later-stage weapon models, first introducing stage, ammunition counts, shot and burst timing, damage, enemy roles, pickups, resupplies, routes, artwork, sound, and encounters remain future content and Combat Lab decisions. The advanced-weapon system model is confirmed.

### 7.4 Later-stage mounted and vehicle encounters

- **Confirmed:** Horses, motorbikes, wagons, and comparable riding systems are **post-Stage 1 authored encounters**, not universal free-roaming systems or pre-stage equipment. Stage 1 may reserve schema extension points or use optional placeholder Lab tests, but mounts are not part of its acceptance scope.
- **Confirmed:** Mounted encounters use shared abstract controls plus an authored profile. Directions steer; `T` attacks; `Z` jumps or performs an evasive maneuver where supported; `G` performs the profile's defense; `U` performs a mounted Special; and a compatible full-meter `H` performs a mounted Ultimate.
- **Confirmed:** A **Chase** profile assists forward motion and emphasizes lane, pace, and lateral adjustment. An **Arena** profile permits bounded free movement. - **Confirmed:** Every mount supplies shared movement, collision, defense, and basic attack rules. - **Confirmed:** Hero Health, Defense, and Attack Bar keep their exact values across mounting and dismounting. A mount or vehicle adds the only new encounter resource, **Stability**.
- **Confirmed:** Direct attacks declare whether they affect Hero Health, Defense pressure, Stability, or a readable combination. Collisions primarily reduce Stability; dangerous crashes may also damage Health. Stability can recover through clean riding, defense, evasion, or a short damage-free interval according to the encounter profile.

- **Confirmed:** Mounted `G` may map to a Defense-consuming vehicle guard or brace, a committed movement-based evasion, or an explicitly compatible Exact Block or Counter. It never changes a universal timing window silently.
- **Confirmed:** Mounted attacks build the normal Attack Bar and a compatible `H` consumes the full bar. Mounting never refills Health, Defense, or Attack Bar.
- **Confirmed:** Separate riders own separate Stability values. A shared vehicle owns one shared Stability value visible to both players. Checkpoints save roles, mount state, Stability, objectives, and the exact persistent hero resources.
- **Confirmed:** Co-op encounter configurations are authored as **Parallel Riders**, **Driver and Fighter**, or **Escort Formation**. Role changes occur only through safe authored transitions or a deliberate swap command, and both roles receive meaningful mechanics, scoring opportunity, and feedback.
- **Confirmed:** Single-player uses deterministic companion behavior, contextual commands, or designated role switching. Same-device co-op always permits both players to act simultaneously; local-Wi-Fi uses the same canonical vehicle state and recovery protocol.
- **Confirmed:** A crash or disconnect by one participant does not automatically fail the other. Encounters provide an authored catch-up, rescue, survival proxy, or checkpoint result consistent with the existing interruption rules.
- **Confirmed:** Mounts enter through story-authored temporary access such as borrowed horses, captured motorbikes, or a defended wagon. Mount and dismount points are clearly marked. Mounts cannot occupy hero loadout slots or be used in unsupported areas.
- **Confirmed:** Store content may provide cosmetic mount variants or explicitly balanced riding-sidegrade content, but never permanent access that bypasses authored encounter boundaries.

- **Confirmed:** Authored group attacks reserve a larger combined budget for every participant and use a distinct shared telegraph. Other major attacks cannot overlap that sequence unless the encounter explicitly provides compatible counterplay.
- **Confirmed:** Co-op encounters enforce both a global encounter budget and per-hero pressure limits so the enemy group cannot arbitrarily spend all pressure on one player. Player-count scaling adjusts the budget deliberately rather than simply doubling it.
- **Confirmed:** Boss encounters may reserve part of the threat budget for the boss while supporting enemies share the remainder. Difficulty may tune budget, recovery, role selection, and move availability, but enemies never read player inputs.
- **Confirmed:** Attack permission and ties resolve from deterministic simulation state, preserving identical crowd behavior across solo, hot-seat, and local-Wi-Fi play.
- **Confirmed:** Co-op targeting uses **Role-Aware Target Scoring with Commitment**. Each enemy scores valid heroes using distance and path availability, tactical role, current pressure distribution, recent interactions, weapon suitability, and encounter objectives.
- **Confirmed:** Reaching a hero's per-hero pressure limit strongly penalizes additional targeting rather than enforcing an artificial equal split. Pressure and grappler roles favor reachable close targets, flankers favor open approach angles, ranged roles favor clear lanes, and support roles favor useful positions around allies.
- **Confirmed:** Recent damage may influence target selection but never creates a universal forced-retaliation rule. Low health also does not automatically attract every enemy; only explicitly authored hunter-style behavior may give vulnerability additional weight.
- **Confirmed:** After selection, an enemy maintains a minimum target-commitment period to prevent rapid switching. It cannot switch during a committed attack, combo, grab, or recovery unless the current target becomes invalid.
- **Confirmed:** Commitment may end early when the target is defeated, disconnected, unreachable, outside the combat region, or no longer compatible with the enemy's current role. Confirmed protected re-entry and other invulnerable states are respected when choosing legal attacks.
- **Confirmed:** Target changes are communicated through facing and movement before the next committed attack. Boss phases may adjust scoring priorities but obey the same valid-target and commitment rules.
- **Confirmed:** Target scoring runs at fixed simulation intervals and uses stable tie-breakers, preserving deterministic behavior in solo, hot-seat, and local-Wi-Fi play.
- **Confirmed:** Enemy defense uses **Tiered Capability Profiles with Shared Rules**. Every enemy participates in the same combat state machine, legal windows, resource costs, anti-infinite protections, and response vocabulary, but each archetype receives an intentional subset of defensive abilities.
- **Confirmed:** Basic enemies normally receive Normal Block plus one simple recovery behavior. Defensive specialists may receive stronger directional guard and Exact Block; counter specialists receive a clearly telegraphed Counter family rather than every possible response.
- **Confirmed:** Grapplers, ranged enemies, weapon users, and other tactical roles receive defenses that reinforce their identity. Elites may combine several advanced options, constrained by Defense resources, recovery commitments, and authored cooldowns.
- **Confirmed:** Bosses may use the broadest defensive toolkit, but access changes visibly by phase and never bypasses normal resource costs or legal activation windows.
- **Confirmed:** Enemy Combo Breakers consume Defense and obey the same state gates, anti-infinite rules, and committed recovery as hero breakers. Capability profiles and cooldowns prevent enemies from chaining Exact Block, Counter, and Breaker without a punishable opening.
- **Confirmed:** Defensive AI uses an authored perception delay and current visible action state; it never reads future player inputs. Difficulty may improve decision quality and reduce reaction delay only within authored fair limits, not grant impossible frame-perfect knowledge.
- **Confirmed:** A compact Defense indicator appears when a non-boss enemy can actively spend defensive resources; boss Defense remains persistently readable. Academy and Combat Lab identify each archetype's defensive capability profile.
- **Confirmed:** Defensive choices, resource use, and cooldowns resolve deterministically rather than through unsynchronized random rolls.
- **Confirmed:** Bosses prevent indefinite stun-locking through a **Visible Resolve Gauge with Earned Stagger Windows** while continuing to use the heroes' core standing, aerial, ground, defensive, and co-op combat vocabulary.
- **Confirmed:** Valid hits deal ordinary Health damage plus authored Resolve damage. Heavy finishers, Counters, Specials, Supers, weapon clashes, and co-op techniques may receive higher Resolve damage values.
- **Confirmed:** While Resolve remains, the boss still shows hit impact and limited reactions but resists full launch, extended ground routes, and submission states unless an action explicitly overrides that resistance.

- **Confirmed:** Boss-specific juggle, stun, bounce, and damage-scaling budgets remain active during Stagger Break. The window ends through its authored duration, exhausted combo budget, or a valid concluding finisher and knockdown.
- **Confirmed:** Stagger Break suppresses autonomous boss Combo Breakers by default so the earned opening remains meaningful. Any rare phase-specific exception must be authored explicitly and telegraphed before it can occur.
- **Confirmed:** After Stagger Break recovery, the boss receives brief protected re-entry and refills Resolve according to the current phase. Resolve never secretly refills while the boss remains in an active combo.
- **Confirmed:** Phase changes may alter Resolve capacity or resistance tags, but HUD state and reactions communicate those changes. Co-op attacks may build and exploit the same Stagger Break without bypassing team-combo limits.
- **Confirmed:** Resolve damage, depletion, recovery, and all state transitions are deterministic and exposed in Combat Lab diagnostics.
- **Confirmed:** Boss phase thresholds use a **Queued Transition with Capped Damage Carryover**. The threshold-crossing hit resolves completely and marks the boss `phasePending` instead of interrupting the current contact or granting immediate invulnerability.
- **Confirmed:** A pending boss remains in its current legal hit reactions until the combo naturally ends, a valid finisher or knockdown concludes it, or an authored maximum transition-grace limit is reached. Health is clamped at the current phase floor during this period.
- **Confirmed:** Additional valid damage accumulates in a visible carryover pool up to an authored cap that cannot reach the following phase threshold. When the pool is full, later contacts may finish the route but do not deal health damage or generate exploitable self-refunding Attack meter.
- **Confirmed:** At the first legal safe point, the transition begins and stored damage is applied when the next combat phase becomes active. If the grace limit expires during an exceptional route, the current hit still resolves before a telegraphed forced separation.
- **Confirmed:** Both heroes enter safe actionable states before the new phase starts. Supers and co-op finishers spend their resources normally even when they trigger a threshold.
- **Confirmed:** Active hazards, projectiles, grabbed weapons, loose pickups, and supporting enemies use explicit phase-transition policies rather than being silently deleted or reset. The complete threshold, carryover, and transition sequence is deterministic in every multiplayer mode.
- **Confirmed:** Enemy attack initiation uses **Visibility-Gated Attacks with Authored Warnings**. The simulation defines an authoritative combat visibility zone in world coordinates, so legality never depends directly on browser resolution or CSS viewport size.
- **Confirmed:** Melee attacks, grabs, sudden dashes, and untelegraphed launchers must begin inside the combat-safe portion of that zone. Hidden enemies may reposition, pursue, collect weapons, and prepare entrances without beginning those offensive actions.
- **Confirmed:** Ranged attacks, reinforcements, and arena-wide boss techniques may begin outside the zone only when explicitly tagged `offscreenCapable`; each requires an authored minimum warning plus a directional edge indicator and distinct audio cue using non-color-only communication.
- **Confirmed:** An attack begun legally while visible may continue if momentum carries its actor partly outside the zone, but it cannot gain new tracking or reverse toward a hero while hidden. Newly spawned enemies cannot arrive with an active damaging hitbox unless the entrance is itself a clearly telegraphed stage hazard.
- **Confirmed:** Off-screen-capable actions reserve their complete threat-budget cost. Boss arena attacks may use wider authored visibility rules, but their danger regions and valid defensive responses remain readable.
- **Confirmed:** Every client renders warnings from the same simulation-defined zone even when display crops differ. Eligibility, warning timing, and resolution remain deterministic across solo, hot-seat, and local-Wi-Fi play.

- **Confirmed:** Cancel timing must remain dependable across supported keyboard and gamepad input and normal browser-performance variation.

- **Confirmed:** Inputs made before the early buffer, after the cancel window, toward an unlinked destination, or without satisfying the move's hit, guard, or whiff condition are discarded instead of becoming delayed standalone attacks.
- **Confirmed:** The Combat Lab and Academy show a specific rejection reason for invalid cancels. Normal combat communicates rejection through subtle audiovisual feedback without diagnostic text interrupting play.
- **Confirmed:** An invalid cancel never replaces an already queued valid action.

- **Confirmed:** Supers remain a destination that requires and consumes a full attack meter; they do not add a second cancel surcharge.
- **Confirmed:** Defense meter never pays for offensive cancel extensions. It remains reserved for guarding and combo breakers.
- **Confirmed:** Cancel costs are committed atomically when a valid transition actually begins. Invalid routes, unmet contact conditions, closed timing windows, and insufficient-meter attempts consume nothing.

- **Confirmed:** Neither a neutral dash nor a dash cancel grants invulnerability; Guard and the defensive system retain that role.
- **Confirmed:** Continuous combos use Target Context plus Tagged Continuations. Every defending combatant owns an independent combo context, covering heroes, ordinary enemies, and bosses under the same lifecycle rules.
- **Confirmed:** A combo context begins with the first confirmed hit against that target and continues while the target remains non-actionable through hit-stun, juggle, or another valid combat state.
- **Confirmed:** Explicit move-data tags may bridge an approved state seam for a ground follow-up, dash chase, or cooperative handoff. A universal grace timer does not preserve a combo after the target has regained actionable control.
- **Confirmed:** A combo ends when the target becomes actionable without a valid continuation, an authored continuation window expires, protected recovery begins, a hero activates a combo breaker, or a boss activates its resolve burst.
- **Confirmed:** Changing targets creates a separate combo context. In co-op, both heroes contribute to the same context when attacking the same target, while individual damage and participation remain trackable.
- **Confirmed:** Closing a combo context resets its dash-bridge allowance, breaker allowance, hit-stun decay, damage scaling, juggle count, knockdown or ground-follow-up limits, and other per-combo state.
- **Confirmed:** Academy recording and combo validation use the same lifecycle boundary. Ending a target's context closes the current recipe sequence; a later hit begins a new sequence.
- **Confirmed:** Hit-stun decay uses a Move-Weighted Stun Budget stored inside each target's combo context. It is an internal combat value rather than a new player-facing resource meter.
- **Confirmed:** Every target context begins with the same provisional 100-point hit-stun budget. Each offensive action spends an authored `stunDecayCost`, allowing light attacks, launchers, specials, ground follow-ups, weapons, and cooperative attacks to carry appropriate weights.
- **Confirmed:** A multi-hit move normally pays one authored decay cost for the complete offensive action rather than one cost per visual hit. Separately cancellable phases may declare separate costs when they function as distinct actions.

- **Confirmed:** Ending the target's combo context restores the full hit-stun budget. The algorithm applies to heroes, enemies, weapons, bosses, and cooperative attacks; boss resolve and hero combo breakers remain additional independent escape systems.
- **Confirmed:** Hit-stun decay changes control duration, not damage. Damage scaling is refined separately so the two balance effects can be evaluated independently.
- **Confirmed:** Juggle limits use a Move-Weighted Juggle Budget plus State Quotas stored inside each target-owned combo context. The budget is an internal control value rather than another player-facing meter.
- **Confirmed:** Every target context provisionally begins with 100 juggle points. Actions spend an authored `juggleCost` when they launch, hold, redirect, or relaunch an airborne target; light aerial continuations, finishers, relaunchers, wall rebounds, and ground bounces may therefore carry different costs.
- **Confirmed:** A multi-hit aerial move normally pays one juggle cost for the complete offensive action rather than one cost per visual hit. Separately cancellable phases may declare separate costs when they behave as distinct actions.
- **Provisional:** From 61 through 100 remaining juggle points, moves retain their full authored lift and trajectory; from 31 through 60 they produce reduced lift and air hold; from 1 through 30 they produce minimal lift with stronger downward momentum.
- **Confirmed:** When an action exhausts the juggle budget, that action still connects and deals damage, but it concludes with protected landing or air recovery. Further aerial cancels and cooperative handoffs close, and the target cannot be relaunched before protected recovery ends.
- **Provisional:** A target combo permits one ordinary wall rebound and one ordinary ground bounce. The same bounce state cannot be alternated repeatedly; authored Super or team-finisher exceptions conclude the combo rather than refreshing it.
- **Confirmed:** Cooperative attackers share the defending target's juggle budget and state quotas, so changing attackers never refreshes airborne control. Changing targets uses the other target's independent context.
- **Confirmed:** Heroes, ordinary enemies, weapons, and bosses use the same juggle-control algorithm. Large enemies and bosses may reject launch reactions except during authored vulnerable states.
- **Confirmed:** Juggle budget, hit-stun budget, and damage scaling remain independent. A move may separately define damage proration, control-duration cost, and airborne-control cost.
- **Confirmed:** Damage scaling uses Move-Level Proration plus a Damage Floor inside each target-owned combo context. A new target context begins at 100% damage scale, and the scale resets only when that context ends.
- **Confirmed:** The opening move may apply authored starter proration to later actions. Fast or safe starters can preserve less future damage than riskier punish, counter, or commitment-heavy starters.
- **Confirmed:** Each offensive action carries an authored `damageScaleCost` that reduces the scale applied to the next action, supporting character-specific normals, aerials, ground attacks, specials, weapons, enemy attacks, boss attacks, and cooperative moves under one algorithm.
- **Confirmed:** A multi-hit move locks one damage scale for its complete authored move damage and distributes that scaled total across its visual hits. Visual hit count alone does not repeatedly lower the scale.
- **Provisional:** Ordinary moves have a global 30% damage floor. Supers and cinematic cooperative finishers may declare their own minimum floors, but they never ignore damage scaling entirely.
- **Confirmed:** Cooperative attackers share the defending target's damage scale, so handing a combo between heroes or alternating attackers cannot reset scaling. Changing targets uses the other target's independent combo context.
- **Confirmed:** Damage is calculated from the move-level scaled total to avoid per-hit rounding drift. Damage scaling affects life damage only; hit-stun decay, meter gain, juggle limits, and continuation validity remain independent systems.
- **Confirmed:** Difficulty may tune health, defense, behavior, and authored move data, but does not silently disable the universal damage-scaling algorithm.

- **Unresolved:** Final juggle-budget size, per-action costs, reaction bands, bounce quotas, and target-specific launch eligibility remain tuning values. The Move-Weighted Juggle Budget plus State Quotas model itself is confirmed.

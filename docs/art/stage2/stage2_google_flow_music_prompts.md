# Fight for Peace — Stage 2 Google Flow Music Prompt Catalogue

**Version:** 1.0 production draft  
**Musical identity:** *Prosperity Under Siege*  
**Use:** Generate candidate material in Flow Music, then edit, loop, mix, and verify rights before game integration.

## 1. Shared musical language

All seven cues belong to one family:

- Warm plucked and sustained strings for village continuity.
- Wooden and hand percussion for work, community, and movement.
- Airy flute or breath-like textures for Wind Sentinel.
- Low frame drums and controlled metallic impacts for Fire Warden.
- Processed water drops, pump pulses, aeration bubbles, and light glass resonance for the greenhouse identity.
- Restrained bass and modern electronic pulse for new technology.
- Dark low strings and distorted metal rhythm for raider pressure and Marek.

Shared exclusions to append to every request:

> Instrumental only, no lyrics, no choir words, no recognizable copyrighted melody, no imitation of a named artist, composer, film, anime, or game soundtrack, no generic triumphant trailer braams, no harsh mastering, no clipped transients, no abrupt ending, no gunshots or combat SFX embedded in the music, no long exposed solo that prevents looping. Leave frequency space for dialogue, captions, objective warnings, impacts, pumps, and environmental ambience.

Request clean endings and sustained tails where the interface allows them. Generate multiple candidates; do not assume the first result is final.

## 2. Cue 1 — Water Became a Future

**Use:** Prosperity Interlude; river restoration through civil-protection formation.

**Flow Music prompt:**

> Create an instrumental cinematic game cue about a rural community turning restored water into a hopeful future. Begin intimate and reflective with gentle plucked strings, soft wooden percussion, distant airy flute, and delicate water-drop rhythm. Gradually add warm sustained strings, hand percussion, subtle pump-like pulse, light glass resonance, and restrained modern bass as reservoirs, greenhouses, aquaponics, school, clinic, and civic life emerge. In the final third introduce disciplined low frame drum and brief pale, breath-like flute gestures for the new civil-protection heroes. Build to earned communal confidence, not conquest. End with a warm unresolved horizon that can crossfade into a quieter patrol cue. Instrumental, emotionally sincere, organic and modern, moderate tempo around 84–92 BPM, 2–3 minutes of editable material.

**Edit needs:** Intro bed, development layer, civil-protection lift, final panorama, 4–8 bar loopable middle, clean transition tail.

## 3. Cue 2 — Roadside Vigil

**Use:** Patrol, staged rescue, clinic arrival, Tomas's deception, and suspicion.

**Flow Music prompt:**

> Create a restrained instrumental game cue for an evening civil-protection patrol that becomes a compassionate rescue and then a quiet mystery. Use muted plucked strings, sparse hand percussion, soft low pulse, breath-like flute fragments, and occasional glass harmonics. The opening should feel observant and safe; introduce an uneven two-note motif when the injured traveler appears, sympathetic at first but subtly ambiguous on repetition. Keep the middle spacious for dialogue and clinic ambience. Add quiet low strings and a barely perceptible mechanical rhythm as the elder notices questions about roads and structures. Tension should come from withheld resolution, not horror. Provide seamless low-intensity looping material and a short suspicious ending sting, around 76–84 BPM.

**Edit needs:** Patrol loop, compassion variation, observation loop, suspicion sting, dialogue-safe mix.

## 4. Cue 3 — Glass and Green

**Use:** Greenhouse exploration, aquaculture, optional branches, and noncombat recovery.

**Flow Music prompt:**

> Create a calm exploratory instrumental cue for a thriving greenhouse and aquaponic complex. Build a transparent texture from marimba-like wooden tones, soft pizzicato strings, airy flute, subtle water droplets, gentle aeration pulse, glass resonance, and a restrained electronic bass that suggests modern systems without science-fiction. The music should feel alive, precise, and cared for. Write a memorable but unobtrusive four- or eight-bar motif that supports exploration and can loop cleanly. Include small harmonic openings for discovery, worker rescue, seed preservation, and medicinal research, while leaving room for ambience and spoken guidance. Avoid spa music and avoid excessive whimsy. Moderate 88–96 BPM, light dynamic range, 2 minutes or more of editable material.

**Edit needs:** Base exploration loop, discovery lift, branch-complete stinger, subtle endangered-system variant without changing tempo.

## 5. Cue 4 — Alarm Through the Channels

**Use:** Ordinary combat, raid escalation, enemy formations, and transitions into Preservation danger.

**Flow Music prompt:**

> Create a loop-oriented instrumental action cue for defending a living greenhouse complex during a coordinated raid. Combine urgent hand percussion, low frame drums, plucked-string ostinato, controlled metallic impacts, restrained electronic bass pulse, short airy flute gestures, and processed water/pump rhythm. The groove must feel tactical and readable rather than frantic. Use clear four- and eight-bar boundaries for safe state changes. Build pressure through interlocking rhythm and darker low strings, not constant loudness. Leave space for attacks, warnings, dialogue, firearms, and machinery. The heroic tone is protection and discipline, not vengeance. Target approximately 112–120 BPM with a stable pulse and at least 90 seconds of clean loopable combat material.

**Edit needs:** Entry sting, base combat loop, elite formation layer, clean loop points, victory release, compatible key/tempo with Cue 5.

## 6. Cue 5 — Protect the Living System

**Use:** Preservation danger layer or alternate loop during sabotage, fires, pump failure, aquatic risk, and Scorched Claim pressure.

**Flow Music prompt:**

> Create an instrumental danger layer designed to combine with or replace a tactical action cue at about 116 BPM. Center the sound on insistent pump-like pulse, tense water-drop rhythm, filtered glass resonance, low bowed-string pressure, restrained metallic ticking, and sparse frame-drum accents. It must communicate that crops, aquatic life, workers, or machinery need attention, without sounding like a countdown to unavoidable failure. Keep harmony compatible with a minor-modal action cue and write at clear four-bar boundaries. Preserve space for alarms and captions. Include a recoverable resolution phrase that relaxes when players stabilize the system. No melody that competes with the main combat theme.

**Edit needs:** Stem-like pressure layer, standalone premix for constrained devices, warning ramp, stabilization release, failure-consequence sting that is serious but not catastrophic.

## 7. Cue 6 — The Gilded Jackal

**Use:** One coherent Marek boss family. Generate the states separately with the same motif, tempo grid, and harmonic center so they can transition cleanly.

### 6A. Mounted pursuit

> Create the opening state of an instrumental boss theme for a ruthless but human raider commander on a civilian motorbike pursuit. Establish a sharp three-note jackal motif in low strings and controlled metal percussion over hand drums and a steady modern bass pulse. Add road momentum without becoming racing music. Keep the tempo around 124 BPM, with readable four-bar phrases and space for engine ambience and mounted warnings. The heroes pursue to protect a community, not for revenge.

### 6B. Armed command

> Continue the exact same three-note motif, tempo, and harmonic center for a boss phase built around finite burst-command pressure and subordinate formations. Add dry metallic rhythm, low frame drums, clipped string figures, and restrained distorted texture. Use gaps and call-and-response to make firing and command warnings audible. Do not embed gunshots. The commander sounds calculating, wealthy, and prepared, but not supernatural.

### 6C. Predator Unbound

> Continue the same motif at the same tempo for the disarmed commander's close-range counter-brawler phase. Shift emphasis from metallic machinery to physical hand percussion, aggressive low strings, and disciplined rhythmic stops. Let the motif fragment and answer itself like a fighter adapting under pressure. Increase intensity through syncopation and harmonic tension, not wall-to-wall volume. Preserve clear loop and transition points.

### 6D. Scorched Claim and surrender

> Transform the same boss motif into a desperate final state where the commander threatens protected systems as his control collapses. Add tense glass and pump textures from the Preservation theme beneath the low strings and drums. Keep danger bounded and readable, not apocalyptic. Provide two compatible endings: a controlled Resolve-surrender release with the motif slowing and losing its metallic edge, and a harder forced-capture cadence that remains restrained and non-executive. Both must converge toward accountability rather than death or triumphal vengeance.

**Edit needs:** Shared motif and tempo map, mounted intro, armed loop, disarm stinger, counter-brawler loop, Preservation overlay, clean-surrender ending, forced-capture ending.

## 8. Cue 7 — What We Protected

**Use:** Community recovery, custody, Tomas's expulsion, results, and sponsor-mystery coda.

**Flow Music prompt:**

> Create an instrumental closing cue about a community recovering after defending people, knowledge, and living systems. Begin quietly with warm strings, wooden tones, water and aeration textures, and a softened version of the Prosperity motif. Add gentle flute and hand percussion as pumps restart, workers repair greenhouses, and prisoners receive documented lawful treatment. For Tomas's expulsion, briefly introduce the ambiguous two-note Roadside motif without cruelty or forgiveness. Let the community theme return with earned warmth. In the final section, place the three-note jackal/sponsor shape far in the background over cool low strings as scouts find an abandoned depot, ending with calm unresolved curiosity rather than a villain reveal. Around 78–88 BPM, dialogue-safe, 2–3 minutes with editable chapter boundaries.

**Edit needs:** Recovery bed, justice variation, expulsion passage, results loop, sponsor-coda sting, warm ending tail and unresolved alternate tail.

## 9. Post-production and rights checklist

- Keep the prompt, generation date, service/model, account/license evidence, candidate ID, exports, edits, and hashes in the asset manifest.
- Confirm commercial-use terms applicable on the generation date and account plan before shipping.
- Screen candidates for accidental similarity to recognizable music; reject doubtful material.
- Human-edit selected material into loops, transitions, stingers, and intensity states.
- Align Cues 4 and 5 and all Cue 6 states to verified tempo grids and compatible harmonic centers.
- Remove embedded noise that could be mistaken for warnings, speech, gunfire, or gameplay SFX.
- Normalize loudness consistently while retaining headroom for combat and accessibility cues.
- Export browser-ready compressed delivery files plus archival masters.
- Test loop seams in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari and through pause, visibility loss, checkpoint reload, and rollback-safe presentation events.
- Provide premixed alternatives for devices that cannot sustain simultaneous adaptive layers.
- Never make music timing part of deterministic combat or scoring state.
